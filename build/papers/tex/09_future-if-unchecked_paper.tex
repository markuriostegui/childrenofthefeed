\subsection{Main Analysis}\label{main-analysis}

The GPTs paper and the IMF note both support an uneven future in which
exposure to generative AI is distributed asymmetrically across
occupations, income positions, and labor systems. The future problem is
not uniform extinction. It is differential vulnerability. \footnote{Sources:
  arXiv, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130}{GPTs are GPTs: An Early
  Look at the L\ldots{}} (2023-03-17; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a2-gpts-are-gpts-an-early-look-at-the-labor-market-impact-potential-of-large-language-models]{Appendix
  A2}); IMF,
  \href{https://www.imf.org/en/publications/staff-discussion-notes/issues/2024/01/14/gen-ai-artificial-intelligence-and-the-future-of-work-542379}{Gen-AI:
  Artificial Intelligence and t\ldots{}} (2024-01-14; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a4-gen-ai-artificial-intelligence-and-the-future-of-work]{Appendix
  A4})}

Compute-governance research supports the claim that high-end capability
is likely to remain infrastructure-concentrated unless policy changes
the terms of access. That means unequal access to machine intelligence
is already an infrastructure question, not only a talent question.
\footnote{Sources: arXiv,
  \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797}{Computing Power and the
  Governance of\ldots{}} (2024-02-13; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a3-computing-power-and-the-governance-of-artificial-intelligence]{Appendix
  A3})}

The continuity scenario also includes unequal fallback. Some of the same
actors asking institutions and publics to normalize deeper dependence on
AI and platform infrastructure have also publicly described or been
credibly reported as building private redundancy, jurisdictional exit,
and survivability options: Altman's 2016 prep disclosures and New
Zealand fallback, Zuckerberg's Kauai shelter complex, and Thiel's long
association with New Zealand retreat logic and elite-exit politics. That
does not prove foreknowledge, coordinated collapse engineering, or a
hidden command chain. It does show how continuity can stratify not only
access to intelligence, but access to backup, redundancy, and escape
when shared systems fail or become politically untrustworthy.
\footnote{Sources: Tad Friend,
  \href{https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2016/10/10/sam-altmans-manifest-destiny}{Sam

The financial and moral-warning layers also matter. The BIS warned of
fragile capex dynamics and weak near-term returns in the AI buildout,
while Dario Amodei publicly warned that AI-era wealth concentration
could strain democratic society. These are not identical claims, but
together they strengthen the dossier's continuity scenario of hierarchy
rather than broad emancipation. \footnote{Sources: Bank for

Interpretation: if current platform habits, labor structures, weights
enclosure, compute chokepoints, and asymmetric fallback systems continue
reinforcing one another, the likely future is not total machine takeover
but a regime of dependency, access hierarchy, and weaker cognitive
sovereignty. A contextual formulation from Dion Wiggins is useful here:
continuity can disguise dependency when systems still appear to function
while effective authority migrates elsewhere. That is precisely why the
dossier's three reforms belong together rather than as isolated policy
tweaks. \footnote{Sources: FTC,
