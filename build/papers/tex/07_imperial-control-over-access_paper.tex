\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}

The AI system is not simply a market of competing products. The United
States has already moved from broad leadership rhetoric into formal
doctrine, consultation processes, and implementation guidance. If
frontier AI is governed through chokepoints, then intelligence itself
becomes a stratified geopolitical infrastructure.

\subsection{Scope and Framing Note}\label{scope-and-framing-note}

This paper examines imperial control over access within the broader
dossier \emph{From Surveillance Capitalism to AI Imperialism}. It is
intentionally written to circulate on its own: necessary context is
restated, evidence boundaries are made explicit, and cited material is
reproduced in paper-local form rather than delegated to repo navigation.
It contributes to the series as a paper that establishes chokepoints,
strategic access, and state-company interfaces that justify anti-capture
obligations. Adjacent reinforcement appears in Chapter 06, Chapter 08,
Chapter 10, but those cross-references are supportive rather than
required for basic comprehension.

\subsection{Central Thesis}\label{central-thesis}

The AI system is not simply a market of competing products. It is also
an architecture of chokepoints. Export controls, cloud dependence, chip
access, and defense-state adjacency increasingly define who can build,
scale, or even reach frontier intelligence. The question is no longer

\subsection{Main Analysis}\label{main-analysis}

BIS guidance gives the advanced-computing export regime an operational
enforcement layer. The politics of AI access is therefore not merely
metaphorical. It is already administrative, technical, and executable.
\footnote{Sources: Bureau of Industry and Security,
  \href{https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/bis-guidance-may-31-2026.pdf}{Guidance
  Regarding Enforcement of Lic\ldots{}} (2026-05-31; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a2-guidance-regarding-enforcement-of-license-requirements-for-advanced-computing-items-for-entities-headquartered-in-country-group-d-5-and-macau]{Appendix
  A2})}

Hyperscaler dependence is also documented. At least one major frontier
lab publicly tied the majority of its workloads and future development
to a single cloud giant, which means frontier capability is already
routed through a small number of infrastructural landlords. \footnote{Sources:
  Amazon,
  \href{https://press.aboutamazon.com/2023/9/amazon-and-anthropic-announce-strategic-collaboration-to-advance-generative-ai}{Amazon
  and Anthropic Announce Strateg\ldots{}} (2023-09-25; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a3-amazon-and-anthropic-announce-strategic-collaboration-to-advance-generative-ai]{Appendix
  A3})}

The formal government-facing layer is now public as well. OpenAI's
\texttt{OpenAI\ for\ Government}, Anthropic's \texttt{Claude\ Gov}, and
the Palantir-AWS-Anthropic classified distribution lane show that
frontier access is organized not only through markets but through
accredited, restricted, and government-priority channels. \footnote{Sources:
  OpenAI,
  \href{https://openai.com/global-affairs/introducing-openai-for-government/}{Introducing

The personnel interface is explicit too. The Army's

\subsection{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

If frontier AI is governed through chokepoints, then intelligence itself
becomes a stratified geopolitical infrastructure. But chokepoints alone
do not explain public acceptance. In the architecture of this series,
Chapter 07 therefore functions as a self-contained argument while also
advancing the cumulative path toward the dossier's three reforms.
