\subsection{Scope and Framing Note}\label{scope-and-framing-note}

This paper examines covid as a historical accelerator within the broader
dossier \emph{From Surveillance Capitalism to AI Imperialism}. It is
intentionally written to circulate on its own: necessary context is
restated, evidence boundaries are made explicit, and cited material is
reproduced in paper-local form rather than delegated to repo navigation.
It contributes to the series as a paper that shows how crisis conditions
accelerated digital dependence and normalized the infrastructure later
challenged by the reform agenda. Adjacent reinforcement appears in
Chapter 01, Chapter 02, Chapter 04, but those cross-references are
supportive rather than required for basic comprehension.

\subsection{Historical or Institutional

\subsection{Main Analysis}\label{main-analysis}

The evidence for acceleration is much stronger. Research on
work-from-home durability, remote collaboration, and pandemic-era
privacy risk shows that digital mediation was not merely a temporary
improvisation. It reshaped institutional life and normalized new forms
of oversight and dependence. \footnote{Sources: arXiv,
  \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11136}{Work-From-Home is Here to
  Stay: Call\ldots{}} (2022-03-21; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a4-work-from-home-is-here-to-stay-call-for-flexibility-in-post-pandemic-work-policies]{Appendix

  Home Affects Collaborat\ldots{}} (2020-07-30; see

The social layer widened as well. COVID-era research and reporting show
that digital platforms became central to health information,
misinformation management, social interpretation, and everyday family
life. Household and youth screen-time evidence makes that
intensification measurable rather than anecdotal. \footnote{Sources:
  arXiv, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01688}{What social media told

The concentration story also became clearer. Pandemic-era gains for
large tech firms diverged sharply from small-business strain, and
serious reporting documented a temporary reprieve from the pre-COVID
techlash because the same firms under scrutiny had become indispensable
infrastructure for work, school, commerce, and communication.

\subsection{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

COVID widened the field in which screens became necessary, platforms
became indispensable, and digitally mediated life became normalized. By
the time frontier AI arrives at scale, the key precondition is already
in place: the human raw material, and much of the dependence needed to
exploit it, has already been captured. In the architecture of this
series, Chapter 03 therefore functions as a self-contained argument
while also advancing the cumulative path toward the dossier's three
reforms.
