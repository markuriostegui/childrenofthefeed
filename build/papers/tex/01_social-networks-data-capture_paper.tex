\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}

Before AI could be marketed as a new intelligence order, social
platforms had already built the behavioral raw material of that order.
The FTC's 2024 report on social media and video-streaming data practices
gives this chapter its strongest regulatory anchor. Once social life is
reorganized around measurable engagement, extractive targeting, and
recommendation systems, platforms stop looking like passive
infrastructure and start looking like privately governed civic systems.

\subsection{Scope and Framing Note}\label{scope-and-framing-note}

This paper examines social networks as infrastructure for free data
capture within the broader dossier \emph{From Surveillance Capitalism to

\subsection{Historical or Institutional
Context}\label{historical-or-institutional-context}

The FTC's 2024 report on social media and video-streaming data practices
gives this chapter its strongest regulatory anchor. The platform model
is documented as one of extraction, retention, combination, and
monetization, not as a neutral relay of user speech. That record is
already enough to weaken the old fiction that sovereign-scale platforms
are passive intermediaries. \footnote{Sources: FTC,
  \href{https://www.ftc.gov/reports/look-behind-screens-examining-data-practices-social-media-video-streaming-services}{A
  Look Behind the Screens: Examining\ldots{}} (n.d.; see

  A1})}

\subsection{Main Analysis}\label{main-analysis}

The FTC's 2019 Facebook action matters because Cambridge Analytica was
not just a reputational scandal. It became a structural compliance event
with board-level oversight duties and a formal federal record of
deceptive and insufficiently controlled data governance. \footnote{Sources:
  FTC,
  \href{https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/07/ftc-imposes-5-billion-penalty-sweeping-new-privacy-restrictions-facebook}{FTC
  Imposes \$5 Billion Penalty and Sw\ldots{}} (2019-07-24; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a2-ftc-imposes-5-billion-penalty-and-sweeping-new-privacy-restrictions-on-facebook]{Appendix
  A2})}

Facebook's own \texttt{S-1} and later Meta proxy filings document Peter
Thiel as an early investor and a long-lived governance actor. That does
not prove clandestine command. It does prove continuity between platform
surveillance, elite venture governance, and later state-adjacent
technology networks. \footnote{Sources: SEC,
  \href{https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000119312512034517/d287954ds1.htm}{Facebook,
  Inc.~Registration Statement\ldots{}} (2012-02-01; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a3-facebook-inc-registration-statement-on-form-s-1]{Appendix
  A3}); SEC,
  \href{https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680122000043/meta2022definitiveproxysta.htm}{Meta
  Platforms, Inc.~2022 Definitive\ldots{}} (2022-04-08; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a4-meta-platforms-inc-2022-definitive-proxy-statement]{Appendix
  A4})}

Meta's 2021 rebrand and 2023 \texttt{10-K} show that the company
attached a metaverse-centered strategic narrative to a still-dominant
advertising machine. The rebrand did not function as a data-governance
reset. It functioned as narrative redirection while the extraction
engine remained intact and financially central. \footnote{Sources: Meta,
  \href{https://about.fb.com/news/2021/10/facebook-company-is-now-meta/}{The
  Facebook Company Is Now Meta} (2021-10-28; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a5-the-facebook-company-is-now-meta]{Appendix A5});
  U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission,
  \href{https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680124000012/meta-20231231.htm}{Meta
  Platforms, Inc.~2023 Annual Repo\ldots{}} (2024-02-02; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a6-meta-platforms-inc-2023-annual-report-form-10-k]{Appendix
  A6})}

The documented platform record therefore shows more than large-scale
surveillance. It shows social participation being routed through
privately governed interfaces that could rank visibility, shape reach,
and condition economic opportunity. Interpretation: this is the earlier
precedent for the later AI story. Once an intermediary controls the
interface layer, it can influence value without producing the underlying
social life itself. \footnote{Sources: FTC,
  \href{https://www.ftc.gov/reports/look-behind-screens-examining-data-practices-social-media-video-streaming-services}{A
  Look Behind the Screens: Examining\ldots{}} (n.d.; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a1-a-look-behind-the-screens-examining-the-data-practices-of-social-media-and-video-streaming-services]{Appendix

  \href{https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/07/ftc-imposes-5-billion-penalty-sweeping-new-privacy-restrictions-facebook}{FTC
  Imposes \$5 Billion Penalty and Sw\ldots{}} (2019-07-24; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a2-ftc-imposes-5-billion-penalty-and-sweeping-new-privacy-restrictions-on-facebook]{Appendix
  A2}); U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission,
  \href{https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680124000012/meta-20231231.htm}{Meta
  Platforms, Inc.~2023 Annual Repo\ldots{}} (2024-02-02; see
  \hyperref[appendix-a6-meta-platforms-inc-2023-annual-report-form-10-k]{Appendix
  A6})}

Interpretation: Chapter \texttt{01} begins the case for
\texttt{Refactor\ Section\ 230} by showing that reach, design,
targeting, monetization, and recommendation are not incidental platform
features. They are the product. A sovereign-scale platform framework
should therefore track systemic design power rather than cling to
passive-hosting logic built for an earlier internet. \footnote{Sources:

\subsection{Reform Relevance}\label{reform-relevance}

Once social life is reorganized around measurable engagement, extractive
targeting, and recommendation systems, platforms stop looking like
passive infrastructure and start looking like privately governed civic
systems. That is why the dossier's Section \texttt{230} argument begins
here: sovereign-scale platforms should be judged by the harms and
dependencies created by their design, amplification, and monetization
architecture. \footnote{Sources: FTC,

\subsection{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

Once social life is reorganized around measurable engagement, extractive
targeting, and recommendation systems, platforms stop looking like
passive infrastructure and start looking like privately governed civic
systems. The next problem is not only that platforms captured behavior.
In the architecture of this series, Chapter 01 therefore functions as a

the dossier's three reforms.
