Central Thesis

The fight over artificial intelligence is not only a fight over products, model benchmarks, or corporate market share. It is also a fight over who owns the condensed value of collective human cognition. In this dossier, the central question is whether captured human language, art, code, labor, culture, science, and emotion can be turned into privately governed strategic weights without triggering a deeper political challenge. 1

Main Analysis

Paul Lara’s published writing adds the dossier’s second framing line: technofeudalism, debt-heavy buildout, paper valuation, and the smoke-economy vocabulary needed to describe how extraction is financed and politically sold. The dossier uses that line as interpretive framing, not as a substitute for primary evidence. 6

Interpretation: the strongest defensible reading is not that every later chapter proves a single grand conspiracy. It is that a coherent line can be drawn from platform extraction, to social weakening, to pandemic acceleration, to model training, to labor correction, to access chokepoints, and finally to legitimacy and capture. The burden of the dossier is to keep the factual layer and the political interpretation distinct while still arguing forcefully from the evidence. 7

Counterarguments

The opening can overreach if it treats the enclosure argument as already complete in legal doctrine rather than politically and institutionally emergent. The House citation proves relevance, not victory. The Lara framing sharpens the language of the dossier, but it does not replace the need for documentary support chapter by chapter. 8

Limits of the Evidence

The House citation proves relevance, not victory. The Lara framing sharpens the language of the dossier, but it does not replace the need for documentary support chapter by chapter. 9

Reform Relevance

The dossier therefore does not end in a vague call for better ethics. It moves toward three explicit reforms:

  • Refactor Section 230
  • AI Weights as Patrimony of Humanity
  • Limit Government Capture by AI Companies

Interpretation: those reforms are not presented as already enacted or legally settled. They are the dossier’s normative answer to the documented structure of extraction, enclosure, and capture. 10

Conclusion

The dossier therefore does not end in a vague call for better ethics. To test that argument, the dossier has to begin before frontier AI. In the architecture of this series, Chapter 00 therefore functions as a self-contained argument while also advancing the cumulative path toward the dossier’s three reforms.
