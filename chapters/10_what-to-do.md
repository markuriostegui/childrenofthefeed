# Chapter 10: What To Do

## Core Thesis

The response program is no longer an open-ended menu. The dossier now takes a direct three-part stance:

- `Refactor Section 230`
- `AI Weights as Patrimony of Humanity`
- `Limit Government Capture by AI Companies`

Cognitive literacy, digital hygiene, labor organization, competition policy, and public-interest infrastructure remain necessary, but they function as implementation arms of these three reforms rather than as disconnected alternatives. [Sources: `us-ed-ai-future-teaching-learning-2023`, `workers-role-ai-ethics-governance-2021`, `ai-digital-public-infrastructure-2024`]

## Main Evidence

The platform chapters already established that sovereign-scale systems shape exposure, amplification, targeting, monetization, and moderation conditions at mass scale. The training chapters established that frontier capability is built from collectively produced expression under contested legal conditions. The access and legitimacy chapters established that frontier firms already move through policy formation, strategic access regimes, lobbying, procurement channels, and public-private legitimacy structures. This final chapter turns those upstream findings into doctrine. [Sources: `ftc-2024-look-behind-screens`, `hhs-social-media-youth-advisory-2023`, `usco-ai-training-report-2025`, `openai-for-government-2025`, `anthropic-claude-gov-2025`, `army-detachment-201-2025`]

### Refactor Section 230

Documented problem: sovereign-scale platforms do not merely host speech. They structure exposure, optimize amplification, monetize targeting, and govern the conditions under which civic, psychological, and political harm scales. [Sources: `ftc-2024-look-behind-screens`, `hhs-social-media-youth-advisory-2023`, `tiktok-kids-mode-2025`, `pew-social-media-democracy-outlier-2022`]

Interpretation: the legal principle should shift from passive hosting to systemic design responsibility. Refactoring Section `230` does not require treating every user post as if it were authored by the platform. It requires treating sovereign-scale platforms as infrastructures whose reach, design, and monetized amplification create duties commensurate with their power. [Sources: `ftc-2024-look-behind-screens`, `hhs-social-media-youth-advisory-2023`, `waller-anderson-online-polarization-2020`]

Policy direction: obligations should track reach, recommendation architecture, amplification, targeting, monetization, moderation architecture, and measurable systemic harm. Child-safety design duties, recommendation auditability, and sovereign-scale platform-risk obligations fit this framework more naturally than either the old passive-intermediary fiction or blanket publisher liability. [Sources: `tiktok-kids-mode-2025`, `instagram-body-image-dms-2025`, `ftc-2024-look-behind-screens`]

### AI Weights as Patrimony of Humanity

Documented problem: frontier-model capability is built from humanity's accumulated language, art, code, labor, science, and socially filtered judgment under legal conditions that remain actively contested. [Sources: `usco-ai-training-report-2025`, `usco-ai-noi-2023`, `radford-gpt2-webtext-2019`, `ap-reddit-google-ai-training-deal-2024`]

Interpretation: once that structure is clear, frontier weights cannot be treated as ordinary private property by default. The dossier's strongest normative direction is patrimony-oriented governance: public-trust logic, anti-monopoly limits, democratic oversight, interoperability duties, and restrictions on exclusive commercialization. In its strongest form, this doctrine pushes toward AI treated more like civic utility infrastructure: open and as broadly accessible as public-interest governance, safety constraints, and durable shared infrastructure can make it. This is advocated doctrine, not settled law. [Sources: `twinchat-ai-copyright-weights`, `house-ai-task-force-report-2024`, `usco-ai-training-report-2025`, `bartz-anthropic-fair-use-order-2025`]

Policy direction: transparency around material training inputs, anti-enclosure limits for dominant model holders, democratic governance mechanisms, and restrictions on exclusive commercialization where public support is material belong inside this reform. [Sources: `usco-ai-training-report-2025`, `openai-us-ai-action-plan-2025`, `ap-reddit-google-ai-training-deal-2024`]

### Limit Government Capture by AI Companies

Documented problem: frontier firms already participate in policy formation, export-control protection, government-only offerings, classified-environment distribution channels, lobbying, electoral vehicles, and military-adjacent personnel structures. [Sources: `openai-us-ai-action-plan-2025`, `openai-for-government-2025`, `anthropic-claude-gov-2025`, `palantir-anthropic-aws-gov-partnership-2024`, `army-detachment-201-2025`, `axios-anthropic-openai-lobbying-quarter-2026`]

Interpretation: any frontier firm benefiting materially from public funding, procurement, compute support, export-control protection, regulatory protection, or national-security access should face heightened public obligations. Public support should increase transparency duties rather than deepen private enclosure. This is an anti-capture doctrine, not a generic appeal to ethics and not a claim of total hidden control. [Sources: `openai-for-government-2025`, `anthropic-claude-gov-2025`, `fec-leading-the-future-committee-overview-2026`, `dario-amodei-adolescence-of-technology-2026`]

Policy direction: procurement visibility, subsidy transparency, lobbying disclosure, conflict-of-interest rules, revolving-door limits, independent audits, model-risk reporting, and limits on exclusive private benefit from publicly supported AI infrastructure should be the baseline. Where firms are simultaneously building quasi-public intelligence infrastructure and private survivability hedges, the burden for public accountability should rise further: dependency-building should not be paired with private exemption from shared failure without stronger oversight and obligations. [Sources: `ap-ftc-ai-partnerships-inquiry-2024`, `openai-for-government-2025`, `anthropic-claude-gov-2025`, `army-detachment-201-2025`, `newyorker-sam-altman-manifest-destiny-2016`, `wired-zuckerberg-hawaii-compound-2023`, `ft-new-zealand-golden-visa-2025`]

## Counterargument or Limit

This chapter is partly normative by design. The current corpus supports the legitimacy of a three-reform agenda, but not yet a fully implementation-ready statutory blueprint in every domain. That is not a weakness if the distinction remains explicit. [Sources: `workers-role-ai-ethics-governance-2021`, `ai-digital-public-infrastructure-2024`, `ap-ftc-ai-partnerships-inquiry-2024`]

## Political and Social Implication

The essential political choice is whether intelligence infrastructure will remain a privately concentrated empire built on captured social life, or whether societies will recover sovereignty over the terms on which cognition itself is organized. The dossier's answer is explicit: sovereign-scale platform accountability, patrimony-oriented governance of frontier weights, and strong limits on opaque state-corporate capture. [Sources: `ftc-2024-look-behind-screens`, `twinchat-ai-copyright-weights`, `openai-for-government-2025`, `anthropic-claude-gov-2025`]

## Transition

The dossier closes here. Its final wager is that technical imperialism is not yet a fate beyond politics, but it will continue to present itself as inevitable progress unless it is named clearly, bounded carefully, and resisted structurally. [Sources: `twinchat-ai-ruining-your-life`, `dario-amodei-adolescence-of-technology-2026`, `vatican-magnifica-humanitas-2026`]
