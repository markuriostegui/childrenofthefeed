# Chapter 05: Talent, Overhiring, Valuation, and Layoffs

## Core Thesis

The cleanest version of the layoffs story is not that AI suddenly made technical labor obsolete. It is that AI arrived as the legitimizing language for a correction that already had deeper causes: pandemic-era overhiring, higher rates, margin pressure, weaker demand, and the tax treatment of research and software spending. The cuts were not born from one machine. They were narrated through one. [Sources: `irs-notice-2023-63`, `ap-amazon-salesforce-tech-purge-2023`, `meta-year-of-efficiency-2023`, `ft-ai-exuberance-tech-weakness-2024`]

## Main Evidence

Section `174` is a real structural factor. IRS guidance and congressional repair efforts show that domestic research and software expenses were caught in a more punitive amortization regime after 2021, and Congress itself treated the burden as significant enough to attempt reversal. This is not the whole story, but it is not rhetorical decoration either. [Sources: `irs-notice-2023-63`, `congress-hr7024-tax-relief-2024`, `congress-hr7024-summary-2024`, `congress-hr7024-text-2024`]

The post-pandemic overhiring record is also strong. Executive statements and major reporting across Meta, Amazon, and Salesforce show a later turn toward efficiency, focus, and cost discipline after an earlier period of aggressive expansion. [Sources: `meta-year-of-efficiency-2023`, `ap-meta-q4-2022-overhiring`, `ap-meta-q4-2023-year-of-efficiency`, `ap-amazon-salesforce-tech-purge-2023`]

Top-tier reporting ties the broader correction to weaker demand, margin pressure, and investor impatience, not only to AI. That matters because it prevents a monocausal story. The financial correction was already underway before AI became the universal explanation for it. [Sources: `ft-ai-exuberance-tech-weakness-2024`, `axios-tech-layoffs-worker-confidence-2024`]

The BIS annual report adds the macro-financial layer. By June 2026, a major multilateral institution was openly warning that the AI capex race was outrunning earnings and free cash flow, with leverage and financing structures becoming part of the risk profile. The system was promising abundance while leaning on increasingly fragile financial scaffolding. [Sources: `bis-annual-economic-report-2026`]

Interpretation: this is where the dossier's economic thesis sharpens. AI did not invent the correction, but it became the language through which it was justified. The same story that encloses captured human production also helps reclassify labor cuts as technological destiny instead of the outcome of financial structure, policy, and managerial excess. That interpretation supports Chapter `04` by showing how enclosure and labor subordination move together. [Sources: `meta-year-of-efficiency-2023`, `ft-ai-exuberance-tech-weakness-2024`, `bis-annual-economic-report-2026`]

## Counterargument or Limit

This chapter should not imply that executives always lied when they invoked AI. Some reallocation toward AI was clearly real. The stronger, more defensible claim is narrower: AI provided a legitimizing narrative layered on top of a structural correction with multiple preexisting causes. [Sources: `meta-year-of-efficiency-2023`, `ap-amazon-salesforce-tech-purge-2023`]

## Political and Social Implication

If this reading holds, then the AI boom is also a class story. It narrows the protected tier, weakens the technical middle class, and presents downward pressure on labor as the rational price of the future. That strengthens the patrimony argument: a system built from collective human production is being enclosed while many of the humans who made it possible are told the future no longer needs them on equal terms. [Sources: `irs-notice-2023-63`, `bis-annual-economic-report-2026`, `twinchat-ai-copyright-weights`]

## Transition

That contradiction sharpens in the next chapter. The same firms that describe AI as dangerous enough to require elite control also release it aggressively, tier it strategically, and normalize dependence at scale. [Sources: `bis-annual-economic-report-2026`, `meta-year-of-efficiency-2023`]
