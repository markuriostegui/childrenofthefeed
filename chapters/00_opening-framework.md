# Chapter 00: Opening Framework

## Core Thesis

The fight over artificial intelligence is not only a fight over products, model benchmarks, or corporate market share. It is also a fight over who owns the condensed value of collective human cognition. In this dossier, the central question is whether captured human language, art, code, labor, culture, science, and emotion can be turned into privately governed strategic weights without triggering a deeper political challenge. [Sources: `twinchat-ai-copyright-weights`, `twinchat-ai-ruining-your-life`]

This chapter also sets the repo's two writing layers. The files in `chapters/` are the reference-corpus narrative: readable, source-traceable, and explicitly bounded. The files in `papers/` are the durable paper-format outputs: self-contained, numerically cited, linked to a global appendix, and reusable for later publication or adaptation. The two layers serve different audiences, but they defend the same argument. [Sources: `house-ai-task-force-report-2024`, `twinchat-ai-copyright-weights`]

## Main Evidence

By December 17, 2024, the Bipartisan House Task Force on Artificial Intelligence had already recorded the training-data conflict in terms overlapping with the dossier's own framework: reproduction, derivative-work theory, commercialization, consent, credit, and compensation. Footnote `40` on physical page `116` cites Mark Uriostegui's essay directly. That does not prove the argument on the merits, but it does prove institutional uptake. [Sources: `house-ai-task-force-report-2024`, `twinchat-ai-copyright-weights`]

Paul Lara's published writing adds the dossier's second framing line: technofeudalism, debt-heavy buildout, paper valuation, and the smoke-economy vocabulary needed to describe how extraction is financed and politically sold. The dossier uses that line as interpretive framing, not as a substitute for primary evidence. [Sources: `paul-lara-tecnofeudalismo-2026`, `paul-lara-great-ai-debt-2026`, `twinchat-ai-ruining-your-life`]

Interpretation: the strongest defensible reading is not that every later chapter proves a single grand conspiracy. It is that a coherent line can be drawn from platform extraction, to social weakening, to pandemic acceleration, to model training, to labor correction, to access chokepoints, and finally to legitimacy and capture. The burden of the dossier is to keep the factual layer and the political interpretation distinct while still arguing forcefully from the evidence. [Sources: `twinchat-ai-copyright-weights`, `twinchat-ai-ruining-your-life`, `house-ai-task-force-report-2024`]

## Counterargument or Limit

The opening can overreach if it treats the enclosure argument as already complete in legal doctrine rather than politically and institutionally emergent. The House citation proves relevance, not victory. The Lara framing sharpens the language of the dossier, but it does not replace the need for documentary support chapter by chapter. [Sources: `house-ai-task-force-report-2024`, `paul-lara-great-ai-debt-2026`]

## Political and Social Implication

The dossier therefore does not end in a vague call for better ethics. It moves toward three explicit reforms:

- `Refactor Section 230`
- `AI Weights as Patrimony of Humanity`
- `Limit Government Capture by AI Companies`

Interpretation: those reforms are not presented as already enacted or legally settled. They are the dossier's normative answer to the documented structure of extraction, enclosure, and capture. [Sources: `twinchat-ai-copyright-weights`, `twinchat-ai-ruining-your-life`, `house-ai-task-force-report-2024`]

## Transition

To test that argument, the dossier has to begin before frontier AI. It has to begin with the platforms that normalized free behavioral capture and built the social substrate later model systems would exploit. [Sources: `twinchat-ai-ruining-your-life`, `twinchat-ai-copyright-weights`]
