# Title

Power Network, Legitimacy, and Institutional Capture in Frontier AI

## Abstract

This paper argues that frontier firms consolidate power not only through capability but through legitimacy. Governance restructures, elite board design, government-only offerings, lobbying, electoral infrastructure, philanthropy, and moral authority all help normalize extraordinary concentration without requiring a conspiratorial script.[1][2][3][4][5][6][7]

## Keywords

legitimacy; lobbying; governance; philanthropy; institutional capture; OpenAI; Anthropic

## Research Question

How is legitimacy built around frontier AI, and when does ordinary legitimacy work begin to resemble institutional capture?

## Central Thesis

The strongest structural reading is that frontier legitimacy is assembled through governance form, security credentials, policy access, political spending, and operational state interfaces. That documented reinforcement justifies a bounded anti-capture argument without requiring a theory of hidden command.[1][2][4][5]

## Evidence and Method Note

This paper uses governance records, public announcements, lobbying disclosures, election filings, and bounded procedural reporting. Named-individual material is included only when it clarifies mission conflict or legal architecture.[1][3][6]

## Main Analysis

OpenAI's 2023 governance rupture and later board reset show that legitimacy had to be actively rebuilt after crisis, not merely presumed from technical status.[1][2]

Later security-oriented appointments and committee structures demonstrate that institutional credibility was rebuilt through establishment guardianship signals as much as through product claims.[2][3]

Operational state interfaces widened the legitimacy field. Government-only offerings, classified-distribution channels, and military-adjacent personnel structures turn privileged access into routine partnership rather than visible exception.[4][5]

The influence layer expanded by 2026. Lobbying disclosures and FEC-backed political vehicles show legitimacy moving into procedural Washington infrastructure and electoral positioning.[6][7]

The moral layer expanded too. Dario Amodei's public warnings about wealth concentration and the Vatican's 2026 AI encyclical show that elite self-positioning and civilizational authority both became part of the legitimacy field.[8][9]

## Counterarguments

This paper does not prove unified backstage control. Elite overlap, lobbying, and public-private partnership are not the same as secret command. The anti-capture case is stronger when argued from formal interfaces and disclosed influence rather than hidden-intent speculation.[6][7]

## Limits of the Evidence

The record is stronger on formal influence and legitimacy assembly than on clandestine influence. The paper therefore supports a bounded structural account of capture risk rather than a maximal theory of total hidden control.

## Reform Relevance

This chapter is the dossier's second primary base for `Limit Government Capture by AI Companies`. It shows why transparency, conflict-of-interest rules, auditability, procurement visibility, and exclusive-benefit limits are needed before elite legitimacy hardens into normalized AI exceptionalism.[4][6][7]

## Conclusion

The frontier AI order is stabilized through ordinary institutions: boards, policy processes, procurement channels, lobbying systems, philanthropy, and moral language. That is precisely why it must be constrained democratically rather than mythologized either as pure conspiracy or as neutral innovation.[1][4][8]
