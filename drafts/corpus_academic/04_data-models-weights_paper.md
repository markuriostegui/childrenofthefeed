# Title

From Human Data to Models and Weights

## Abstract

This paper argues that foundation-model power rests on captured human expression, labor, and socially filtered judgment. Drawing on Copyright Office materials, court records, memorization research, Reddit-to-WebText evidence, and licensing developments, it shows that the legal and political dispute over AI training is really a dispute over enclosure of collective human production.[1][2][3][4][5][6][7]

## Keywords

training data; copyright; model weights; memorization; patrimony; enclosure

## Research Question

How far does the existing legal and technical record support treating frontier AI weights as a governance problem rather than an ordinary private-property outcome?

## Central Thesis

Foundation models emerge after large-scale capture of human expression and labor. Because the resulting weights condense collectively produced value under actively contested legal conditions, the dossier is justified in treating `AI Weights as Patrimony of Humanity` as a serious normative governance direction.[1][2][3][7]

## Evidence and Method Note

This paper relies on primary legal and policy materials, plus technical evidence and bounded secondary reporting. It distinguishes carefully between documented conflict, unsettled doctrine, and the further interpretive step toward patrimony language.[1][2][4][5]

## Main Analysis

The Copyright Office's 2023 inquiry and 2025 report establish that AI training on copyrighted works is not a settled technical inevitability but a live federal policy dispute involving inputs, disclosure, licensing, and compensation.[1][2]

Technical evidence matters because it undercuts the claim that models are only harmless abstractions from source material. Memorization research showed that large language models can reproduce identifiable training data, and later court records confirmed that training-stage copying and corpus assembly remain central legal issues.[3][4][5]

The social-to-model bridge is explicit. GPT-2's WebText relied on socially filtered outbound links shared on Reddit, and by February 2024 Reddit had moved from diffuse public capture to direct licensing for AI training. The extraction economy thus evolves from unpaid social production to direct enclosure of archived human conversation.[6][7]

Human labor remains inside the system as well. Research on annotation and outsourced AI work shows that model capability depends on undervalued human correction, verification, and support, not only on scraped creative works.[8]

## Counterarguments

This paper should not imply that every frontier-model corpus has already been fully mapped or that patrimony is already recognized doctrine. The stronger claim is that the conflict is real, structurally significant, and no longer reducible to industry metaphor.[1][5]

## Limits of the Evidence

The main remaining limit is provenance breadth rather than absence of conflict. The record is already strong on legal dispute, technical leakage, social-data bridges, and labor externalization, but not on full disclosure of every frontier stack.

## Reform Relevance

This is the dossier's primary evidentiary base for `AI Weights as Patrimony of Humanity`. The paper supports a normative direction toward public-trust logic, democratic oversight, anti-monopoly controls, and broad, utility-like availability of frontier intelligence, while making clear that this remains argued doctrine rather than settled law.[1][2]

## Conclusion

Once civilization itself becomes the training set, ownership of the resulting weights can no longer be treated as a morally neutral default. The real question becomes what public obligations follow from that fact.[1][7]
