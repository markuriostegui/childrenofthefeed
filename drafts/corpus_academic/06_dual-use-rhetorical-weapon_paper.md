# Title

AI as a Dual-Use Rhetorical Weapon

## Abstract

This paper examines the contradiction in which frontier AI is framed as dangerous enough to justify elite control and safe enough to justify broad social dependence. It argues that reasoning advances are real, but that those advances are packaged within a politics of fear, prestige, and tiered access.[1][2][3][4][5][6]

## Keywords

reasoning models; test-time compute; pricing; safety rhetoric; DeepSeek; frontier AI

## Research Question

How should the dossier understand frontier reasoning systems if both the technical progress and the access-control rhetoric are real at the same time?

## Central Thesis

The strongest reading is not that reasoning advances are fake. It is that a real compute-intensive technical mechanism is being governed through rhetoric and pricing that normalize dependency while preserving hierarchy.[1][2][4][5]

## Evidence and Method Note

This paper draws on primary OpenAI and Anthropic material, Reuters reporting on prehistory, technical papers on test-time compute, and public pricing pages. It separates the reality of capability from the politics of capability management.[1][2][3][4][5][6]

## Main Analysis

OpenAI's reasoning materials and related safety framing show that the product story and the security story arrived together. Reasoning was launched not only as a stronger technical capability, but as a capability tied to preparedness and misuse-risk discourse.[1][2]

Reuters reporting on Q* and `Strawberry` shows that the public reasoning story has a real prehistory. This is important because it means the chapter does not need to invent a false capability story in order to critique the governance of that capability.[3]

Primary research on test-time compute strengthens the argument further. A 2024 paper showed that carefully allocated inference-time compute can materially improve efficiency, while open replication and later critique showed both diffusion pressure and an unresolved dispute over what those replications truly capture.[4][5][6]

At the same time, OpenAI and Anthropic pricing records show reasoning capability being commercialized through premium tiers, differentiated plans, and higher-priced API lanes. The political meaning of reasoning is therefore inseparable from stratified access.[7][8]

## Counterarguments

This paper should not be read as a claim that frontier capability is imaginary or that every replication erased genuine frontier advantage. The more defensible claim is about co-produced scarcity: real capability, real diffusion pressure, and real political curation at once.[4][5][6]

## Limits of the Evidence

The evidence is strongest on capability framing, access pricing, and technical dispute. It is weaker on proving a single subsidy formula behind every consumer-facing price point.

## Reform Relevance

This chapter supports `AI Weights as Patrimony of Humanity` by showing how collectively grounded capability is enclosed as premium, selectively governed access rather than treated as a broadly available public good.[7][8]

## Conclusion

The dual-use contradiction is not that AI is both fake and dangerous. It is that a real technical mechanism is being narrated in ways that legitimize both dependence and concentrated control.[1][2][7]
