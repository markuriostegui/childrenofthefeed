# Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Teaching and Learning: Insights and Recommendations

## Factual Summary

- The U.S. Department of Education's Office of Educational Technology published this report in May 2023 to frame opportunities, risks, and policy recommendations for artificial intelligence in educational settings.
- The report argues that AI changes education not only by expanding access to tools, but by shifting systems toward pattern detection and automated decision-making in teaching, assessment, and administration.
- It emphasizes governance problems around bias, privacy, safety, transparency, human oversight, and educator capacity rather than treating AI in schools as a frictionless efficiency gain.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The report describes a shift from capturing data to detecting patterns in data, and from providing access to instructional resources to automating decisions about instruction and other educational processes.
- It states that educational systems must govern their use of AI systems because pattern detection and automated decisions can encode bias and unfairness.
- The recommendations center human judgment, transparency, training, and public-policy development rather than blind adoption.

## What It Proves

- That a primary U.S. education authority already treated AI-mediated learning as a governance question rather than as a neutral productivity upgrade.
- That institutional guidance had already identified bias, privacy, safety, and over-delegation as central risks in educational AI adoption.
- That Chapter 09 can ground dependency and cognitive-mediation concerns in official educational governance language.

## What It Suggests

- That Chapter 10 can defend cognitive literacy, educator oversight, and institutional guardrails as legitimate response layers rather than lifestyle preferences.
- That the future-dependency argument is strongest when framed around delegated judgment and institutional design, not around panic about any single tool.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that AI has already degraded learning outcomes at population scale.
- It does not prove that every AI-assisted educational use creates passivity or dependency.
- It does not prove that one specific regulatory template has already solved the governance problem.
