# Computing Power and the Governance of Artificial Intelligence

## Factual Summary

- This February 13, 2024 paper argues that computing power is a central input to frontier AI and therefore a core site of governance rather than a neutral background resource.
- The paper treats compute as measurable, excludable, and institutionally chokepointed, which makes it a powerful mechanism for shaping who can train and deploy advanced systems.
- It frames compute governance as materially relevant to both private concentration and state control over the AI stack.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The paper identifies compute as a scarce and governable resource rather than an invisible technical detail.
- It links concentrated access to advanced hardware, cloud infrastructure, and supply chains with downstream control over model capability.
- It argues that compute governance tools can shape development trajectories even when model weights or datasets are harder to monitor directly.

## What It Proves

- That access hierarchy in frontier AI is materially tied to compute concentration and not only to abstract innovation differences.
- That Chapter 09 can speak about unequal access to high-end intelligence as a documented infrastructure problem.
- That the project's language of chokepoints and access stratification has direct support in primary research on compute governance.

## What It Suggests

- That continuity under current conditions is likely to favor actors with privileged access to chips, clouds, and capital-intensive training infrastructure.
- That Chapter 10 should treat public-interest compute and infrastructure policy as substantive response options, not symbolic side issues.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that compute concentration is the only relevant bottleneck in AI power.
- It does not prove that current chokepoints are politically immutable.
- It does not prove that every form of open-model diffusion cancels the importance of compute control.
