# FTC Imposes $5 Billion Penalty and Sweeping New Privacy Restrictions on Facebook

## Factual Summary

- On July 24, 2019, the FTC announced a settlement with Facebook imposing a record $5 billion penalty and a restructuring of internal privacy compliance.
- The FTC said Facebook violated its 2012 order by deceiving users about their ability to control the privacy of their personal information.
- The release also ties the case to the Cambridge Analytica ecosystem and describes new layers of oversight, including an independent board privacy committee and personal certifications.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The release is dated July 24, 2019.
- The FTC says Facebook will pay a record $5 billion penalty.
- The regulator states that Facebook violated the 2012 order by deceiving users about control over their information.
- The FTC also points to separate actions against Cambridge Analytica, Alexander Nix, and Aleksandr Kogan.

## What It Proves

- That there is formal regulatory backing for saying Facebook monetized and managed personal data within a framework of deceptive practices and insufficient oversight.
- That the Cambridge Analytica scandal was not only a media episode, but a point of institutional intervention with structural compliance consequences.
- That Facebook’s commercial-surveillance architecture generated enough risk to require extraordinary board-level oversight mechanisms.

## What It Suggests

- That mass data extraction and opacity about its use were not marginal deviations, but central traits of the platform business model.
- That the later rebrand to Meta can be read, at least in part, against the backdrop of an unresolved reputational and regulatory crisis.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove on its own that the rebrand to Meta was specifically designed to erase Cambridge Analytica.
- It does not prove a direct connection to later AI model training.
- It does not prove state-intelligence participation or a total theory of the Facebook case.
