# OpenAI o1 System Card

## Factual Summary

- Updated on December 5, 2024, OpenAI's `o1` system card presents the model family as reasoning systems trained with large-scale reinforcement learning.
- The card says `o1` and `o1-mini` were trained on a mix of publicly available data, proprietary data accessed through partnerships, and custom in-house datasets.
- It also pairs capability claims with explicit preparedness, red-teaming, and misuse-risk language, including medium-risk findings in some areas.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The page is marked "Updated: December 5, 2024."
- OpenAI says the `o1` family is "trained with large-scale reinforcement learning to reason using chain-of-thought."
- The card says the models were pre-trained on "a mix of publicly available data, proprietary data accessed through partnerships, and custom datasets developed in-house."
- It states that only models with a post-mitigation score of medium or below can be deployed.
- The card says the family represents a transition from fast, intuitive thinking to "slower, more deliberate reasoning."

## What It Proves

- That OpenAI itself paired stronger reasoning capability with formal safety, preparedness, and misuse-risk framing.
- That the company documented a training-data mix combining public web-scale material, proprietary partnership data, and internal datasets.
- That Chapter 06 can ground the coexistence of capability escalation and safety-governance packaging in a primary source.

## What It Suggests

- That safety language functioned as part of the release architecture for frontier reasoning models rather than only as a constraint against deployment.
- That the movement toward proprietary data partnerships is relevant to the broader enclosure and access argument in the dossier.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that OpenAI's internal preparedness process is sufficient or externally validated in every dimension.
- It does not prove that the company released full information about how training data was sourced or licensed.
- It does not prove that "reasoning" models are qualitatively beyond replication by competitors or open derivatives.
