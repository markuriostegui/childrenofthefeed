# Introducing Claude 4

## Factual Summary

- Published on May 22, 2025, Anthropic's `Introducing Claude 4` post launched `Claude Opus 4` and `Claude Sonnet 4` as hybrid models capable of near-instant responses and extended thinking.
- The release explicitly described broad product availability across Claude subscription plans, the Anthropic API, Amazon Bedrock, and Google Cloud Vertex AI.
- It is especially useful to Vector 06 because it links reasoning-style model capability to immediate commercialization, plan segmentation, and listed API prices in one primary launch document.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The release is dated May 22, 2025.
- Anthropic says both models are hybrid systems able to produce near-instant responses or extended thinking.
- The post says `Claude Opus 4` and `Sonnet 4` are available in the API, on Amazon Bedrock, and on Google Cloud's Vertex AI.
- The release says both models are included in Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans, while Sonnet 4 is also available to free users.
- Anthropic lists API pricing for Opus 4 at a much higher level than Sonnet 4, documenting internal product differentiation at launch.

## What It Proves

- That on May 22, 2025 Anthropic launched reasoning-oriented hybrid frontier models together with immediate commercial distribution across subscription plans, API access, and major cloud platforms.
- That Anthropic itself tied extended-thinking capability to differentiated plan availability, with full-model access concentrated in paid tiers even while Sonnet 4 also reached free users.
- That reasoning-capable frontier models were commercialized with explicit API price differentiation at launch rather than released only as a research artifact.

## What It Suggests

- That frontier reasoning products can be marketed simultaneously as a capability milestone and as a commercial platform layer.
- That partial free access does not negate structured premium access to the strongest model family.
- That broad cloud availability can expand reach while still preserving price hierarchy and product gating.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove Anthropic's internal cost to serve extended-thinking workloads or whether launch pricing reflected subsidy.
- It does not prove that Opus 4's price premium maps perfectly onto real-world reasoning advantage in every domain.
- It does not prove that commercial rollout itself invalidates the firm's safety language elsewhere; it only shows that safety rhetoric and broad release can coexist.

## Reliability

- Primary company launch post published by Anthropic on May 22, 2025.
- Reliable for launch-date availability, model framing, distribution channels, and listed API prices.
- As a company release, it should be read as a primary statement of launch terms rather than an independent performance evaluation.
