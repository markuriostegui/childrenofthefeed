# ChatGPT Plans | Free, Go, Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise

## Factual Summary

- Captured on July 3, 2026, OpenAI's current ChatGPT pricing page presents a tiered product ladder from Free through Pro, Business, and Enterprise.
- The page explicitly reserves the highest-end reasoning access for upper paid tiers, including `GPT-5.5 Pro` and larger usage envelopes for `GPT-5 thinking`.
- The source matters to Vector 06 because it shows that reasoning capability is distributed through plan segmentation and usage multipliers rather than through equal public access.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The page describes Pro as including `GPT-5.5 Pro` plus `GPT-5 thinking` and `GPT-5 thinking mini`.
- The page frames Pro as offering `unlimited` access to `GPT-5.5` and `GPT-5 thinking`, while Business and Enterprise describe flexible or expanded access patterns rather than a flat universal tier.
- Lower plans describe access to `GPT-5.5`, `GPT-5 thinking`, or limited message allowances without extending the same top-tier access language to every user level.

## What It Proves

- That as of capture on July 3, 2026, OpenAI's official ChatGPT plan structure reserved the highest-end reasoning access for upper paid tiers rather than offering it uniformly across all users.
- That current official consumer access to reasoning-capable models is stratified by plan, usage envelope, and feature set.
- That Chapter 06 can ground its reasoning-premium argument in a primary access-policy source rather than relying only on commentary about hype or control.

## What It Suggests

- That frontier reasoning is being packaged not just as a technical capability, but as a premium subscription layer with differentiated cognitive access.
- That "widely available AI" can still mean sharply unequal access to the strongest or least constrained reasoning modes.
- That plan design itself belongs to the political economy of frontier AI, not only the models' benchmark performance.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove the internal cost structure behind each plan or whether margins are positive at every tier.
- It does not prove that OpenAI's consumer-tier segmentation alone determines broader market concentration or state access.
- It does not prove that every model listed as available to a lower tier is available with the same throughput, context, or operational freedom as the upper tiers.
