# Pricing | OpenAI API

## Factual Summary

- Captured on July 3, 2026, OpenAI's official developer pricing page lists materially different token rates across its flagship and `Pro` model families.
- The page shows that `GPT-5.5 Pro` is priced far above `GPT-5.5` for both input and output tokens, with the same premium pattern visible in long-context pricing.
- The source is valuable to Vector 06 because it documents a primary cost and access distinction between ordinary flagship use and premium reasoning-oriented use.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The pricing page lists `GPT-5.5` at lower input and output token rates than `GPT-5.5 Pro`, with `GPT-5.5 Pro` carrying a steep premium in both standard and long-context use.
- The page presents the model families together inside one official pricing schedule rather than as unrelated products.
- The page therefore records premium frontier capability as a directly monetized API tier, not only as branding language.

## What It Proves

- That as of July 3, 2026, OpenAI's official API pricing imposed a large price premium on `GPT-5.5 Pro` relative to `GPT-5.5`.
- That OpenAI's current API record treats top-tier reasoning-capable access as materially higher-cost infrastructure rather than as a negligible increment over ordinary flagship use.
- That Chapter 06 can document reasoning-premium monetization through a primary pricing source rather than only through media summaries.

## What It Suggests

- That the market story of "cheap AI" can coexist with steep price differentials once users seek the most capable or least constrained reasoning tier.
- That premium reasoning may function as both a technical and commercial moat, especially when paired with proprietary distribution and compute.
- That later public claims about affordability should be checked against the pricing structure actually offered to developers.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove OpenAI's internal serving cost, subsidy level, or profitability on these models.
- It does not prove that higher price alone corresponds one-to-one with higher risk, better reasoning, or stronger real-world performance.
- It does not prove how much large enterprise customers ultimately pay after discounts or custom commercial terms.
