# Learning to reason with LLMs

## Factual Summary

- On September 12, 2024, OpenAI published a release introducing `o1-preview` as an early reasoning-focused model.
- The release says the model's performance improves with both additional reinforcement learning at training time and more time spent thinking at inference time.
- OpenAI presents benchmark gains over GPT-4o in math, coding, and science as the core justification for the new reasoning-model framing.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The page is dated September 12, 2024.
- OpenAI says `o1` "consistently improves with more reinforcement learning (train-time compute) and with more time spent thinking (test-time compute)."
- The release says `o1` ranks in the 89th percentile on Codeforces, places among the top 500 students in a USA Math Olympiad qualifier, and exceeds human PhD-level accuracy on GPQA.
- The release says `o1-preview` was being made available immediately in ChatGPT and to trusted API users.

## What It Proves

- That OpenAI publicly framed reasoning as a new scaling layer based on additional train-time and test-time compute.
- That the company explicitly marketed `o1` as a major reasoning advance over GPT-4o in math, coding, and science-heavy evaluations.
- That Chapter 06 can document reasoning as a commercial and strategic product category, not just a later media label.

## What It Suggests

- That part of the frontier competition was shifting from pretraining scale alone toward inference-time and reinforcement-learning-heavy differentiation.
- That the language of "thinking" and "reasoning" also functioned as a market narrative for a new premium capability tier.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that benchmark performance translates cleanly into all real-world uses.
- It does not prove that the `o1` release represented a fully discontinuous scientific break rather than a scaling and packaging shift.
- It does not prove that OpenAI alone defined the reasoning paradigm or that rivals could not reproduce parts of it.
