# Notice 2023-63: Guidance on Section 174 and specified research or experimental expenditures

## Factual Summary

- IRS Notice 2023-63 is an official September 8, 2023 notice providing interim guidance on the treatment of specified research or experimental expenditures under Section 174.
- For this dossier, the value of the notice is not a claim about layoffs by itself, but documentary proof that the Section 174 amortization regime was real, material, and administratively active during the post-2022 tech correction.
- The local PDF text extraction failed, so this note stays conservative and relies on the source title, catalog metadata, and the notice's official role as guidance on Section 174 capitalization and amortization.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The notice is officially titled "Guidance on Section 174 and specified research or experimental expenditures."
- It is dated September 8, 2023.
- It is an IRS notice, not commentary or secondary analysis.
- It exists as part of the administrative machinery implementing the post-2021 Section 174 treatment of research costs.

## What It Proves

- That Section 174 amortization was not a rumor or niche accounting theory; it was the subject of active IRS guidance.
- That U.S. firms with research-heavy cost structures had to account for a changed tax treatment of those expenditures.
- That Chapter 5 can legitimately treat Section 174 as one documented structural factor in the financial environment facing software and R&D-heavy companies.

## What It Suggests

- That the tax treatment of software and research spending belongs in any serious explanation of the post-2022 tech correction.
- That later corporate narratives centered on AI efficiency often sat on top of preexisting accounting and financing pressures.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that Section 174 was the sole or dominant cause of layoffs at any specific company.
- It does not prove how any one firm modeled the rule internally.
- It does not prove the broader editorial thesis without support from earnings, reporting, and labor-market evidence.
