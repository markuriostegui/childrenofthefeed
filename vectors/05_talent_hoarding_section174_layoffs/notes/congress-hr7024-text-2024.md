# H.R. 7024 text: Deduction for domestic research and experimental expenditures

## Factual Summary

- The bill text for H.R. 7024 shows the operative language behind the proposed temporary restoration of deductions for domestic research or experimental expenditures.
- The text states that domestic research expenditures would be deductible, while still allowing taxpayers to elect capitalization and amortization over at least 60 months.
- It also explicitly states that software-development expenditures are treated as research or experimental expenditures for this section.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- "there shall be allowed as a deduction any domestic research or experimental expenditures"
- Taxpayers may elect capitalization and a ratable amortization period of "not less than 60 months"
- "any amount paid or incurred in connection with the development of any software shall be treated as a research or experimental expenditure"

## What It Proves

- That the practical Section 174 fight directly implicated software development, not only laboratory research.
- That the legislative response was not vague rhetoric; it proposed specific statutory language restoring domestic deductions and naming software explicitly.
- That Chapter 5 can connect the accounting treatment of software and R&D spending to the financial environment of large tech employers.

## What It Suggests

- That firms with large software payrolls had reason to care materially about the treatment of domestic R&D costs.
- That the Section 174 story belongs in a structural account of tech layoffs even where overhiring and rates remain central.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove the bill became law in the form shown here.
- It does not prove any company cited this bill text internally when planning layoffs.
- It does not by itself establish the size of the effect on any firm's tax bill.
