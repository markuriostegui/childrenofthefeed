# H.R. 7024: Tax Relief for American Families and Workers Act of 2024 (CRS summary)

## Factual Summary

- Congress.gov's CRS-authored summary for H.R. 7024 states that Title II would delay the date on which taxpayers must begin deducting domestic research or experimental costs over a five-year period until 2026.
- The same summary states that taxpayers may expense those domestic research costs incurred between 2022 and 2026.
- This is not commentary about layoffs. It is an official congressional summary showing that Section 174 treatment was a live legislative issue with practical consequences for research-heavy firms.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- "Bill summaries are authored by CRS."
- "This title allows taxpayers to delay the date on which they must begin deducting their domestic research or experimental research costs over a five-year period until 2026."
- "Taxpayers may therefore expense such costs incurred between 2022-2026."

## What It Proves

- That Congress formally treated the post-2021 Section 174 regime as burdensome enough to warrant legislative reversal or postponement.
- That the practical effect of the existing rule can be described in plain English: firms were being pushed into five-year deduction treatment for domestic research costs until the proposed relief would restore expensing through 2025.
- That Chapter 5 can treat Section 174 as a material structural factor in the post-pandemic correction without pretending it was the only cause of layoffs.

## What It Suggests

- That the tax treatment of domestic research costs was important enough to become part of a broader business-pressure story facing software and R&D-heavy companies.
- That claims about Section 174 were not merely retrospective social-media rationalizations.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that any one company laid workers off because of Section 174 alone.
- It does not prove that H.R. 7024 caused the correction, only that Congress recognized a concrete policy burden.
- It does not prove how software firms internally modeled the tax change.
