# H.R. 7024: Tax Relief for American Families and Workers Act of 2024

## Factual Summary

- The Congress.gov page for H.R. 7024 documents a House-passed attempt to delay the Section 174 amortization schedule for domestic research or experimental expenditures.
- The CRS-authored summary states that taxpayers would be able to expense such domestic costs incurred between 2022 and 2026 rather than beginning five-year amortization immediately.
- In the dossier, this source helps show that lawmakers themselves treated the Section 174 change as significant enough to warrant legislative reversal.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The page identifies the bill as H.R. 7024, the Tax Relief for American Families and Workers Act of 2024.
- The summary is shown as "Passed House (01/31/2024)."
- The CRS summary says the title allows taxpayers to delay the date on which they must begin deducting domestic research or experimental research costs over a five-year period until 2026.
- The same summary says taxpayers may therefore expense such costs incurred between 2022 and 2026.

## What It Proves

- That Congress formally moved to soften or reverse the immediate impact of Section 174 on domestic research expensing.
- That the practical consequences of Section 174 were concrete enough to generate a serious legislative response.
- That Chapter 5 can frame Section 174 as a live business issue rather than an after-the-fact excuse invented by commentators.

## What It Suggests

- That companies and policymakers alike viewed the amortization rule as a meaningful drag on research-heavy firms.
- That tax treatment belongs alongside rates, overhiring, and margin pressure in explaining the layoff cycle.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that the bill alone would have changed corporate layoff decisions.
- It does not prove that all firms were equally affected.
