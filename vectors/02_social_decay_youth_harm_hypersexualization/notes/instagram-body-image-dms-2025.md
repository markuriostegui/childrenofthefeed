# Unfiltered: How Teens Engage in Body Image and Shaming Discussions via Instagram Direct Messages (DMs)

## Factual Summary

- This 2025 paper analyzes 1,596 sub-conversations within 451 Instagram DM conversations from 67 teens ages 13 to 17 who discussed body image privately.
- The study finds that teens often received support when sharing negative body-image struggles, but also encountered criticism and body shaming in other exchanges.
- The findings vary by setting: one-on-one DMs more often contained support, while group conversations more often contained negative or body-shaming interactions.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The abstract reports 1,596 sub-conversations across 451 DM conversations from 67 teens.
- It says sharing struggles and receiving support happened most often in one-on-one conversations.
- It says body shaming and negative interactions appeared more often in group settings.
- The authors conclude that private social-media settings significantly influence how teens discuss and respond to body image.

## What It Proves

- That teen body-image discourse on Instagram is not confined to public feeds; it also unfolds in private channels that shape social reinforcement and harm.
- That platform architecture matters: the difference between one-on-one and group settings changes the texture of support, criticism, and shaming.
- That Chapter 2 can discuss mediated body-image norms with stronger empirical grounding than a general moral complaint.

## What It Suggests

- Review causal relationships carefully before elevating them into a thesis.
- That private messaging environments can intensify both affirmation and harm around body-image discourse among minors.
- That platform-driven moral reconfiguration may operate not only through public virality but also through intimate, semi-private interaction spaces.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that Instagram uniquely causes body-image harms rather than reflecting broader cultural pressures.
- It does not prove that all teen Instagram use is harmful; the paper also documents supportive exchanges.
- It does not prove the broader claims about hypersexualization or prostitution glamour without additional evidence.
