# What Do We Mean When We Talk about Trust in Social Media? A Systematic Review

## Factual Summary

- This 2023 systematic review examines how trust in social media is defined, measured, and discussed across 70 papers.
- The authors argue that trust in social media is conceptually fragmented: researchers variously focus on trust in content, users, or platforms, and the field lacks a stable shared definition.
- The paper synthesizes antecedents of trust, the ways trust affects behavior and attitudes, and research gaps that complicate easy claims about platform effects.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The abstract says there is "conflicting evidence about the antecedents and implications of trusting social media content, users, and platforms."
- It also says the field lacks "basic agreement as to what trust means in the context of social media."
- The authors present the review as a synthesis of definitions, measurements, antecedents, impacts on attitudes and behavior, and future directions.

## What It Proves

- That trust in social media is a serious research problem with enough literature to support systematic synthesis.
- That trust in platforms, users, and content should not be treated as interchangeable concepts inside the dossier.
- That Chapter 2 can discuss erosion of trust and social interpretation with scholarly support, while still keeping conceptual precision.

## What It Suggests

- Review causal relationships carefully before elevating them into a thesis.
- That social-media systems can shape not only attention but also epistemic and relational trust.
- That the dossier should treat declining trust as a layered problem involving platforms, users, and mediated information rather than as one flat variable.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that social media uniformly destroys trust in every context.
- It does not prove that trust decline is caused only by algorithms rather than broader institutional and political forces.
- It does not prove the specific moral or cultural claims in Chapter 2 without additional evidence.
