# Nudes? Shouldn't I charge for these?: Motivations of New Sexual Content Creators on OnlyFans

## Factual Summary

- This 2022 paper studies 22 U.S.-based OnlyFans creators who had not previously done sex work.
- The authors find that three major factors shaped entry into sexual content creation on the platform: mainstream visibility and social acceptance, platform affordances around privacy and boundary setting, and pandemic-era opportunity during lockdown disruption.
- The source gives Chapter 2 a more disciplined way to discuss the normalization and commercialization of sexual self-presentation online.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- OnlyFans creators are positioned at the intersection of professional social-media content creation and sex work.
- Participants were motivated by societal visibility and mainstream acceptance of OnlyFans.
- Platform affordances such as privacy from the public, boundary setting, and archived content mattered to adoption.
- The pandemic created an opportunity to overcome lockdown-related issues.

## What It Proves

- That mainstream visibility and platform design helped normalize monetized sexual content creation among people with no prior sex-work history.
- That the pandemic was a documented adoption driver in this transition.
- That Chapter 2 can discuss digital prostitution glamour and normalization with a real research basis rather than relying only on celebrity examples or moral impression.

## What It Suggests

- That sexual-commercial aesthetics online can spread through a mix of culture, platform affordance, and economic pressure.
- That platform-mediated normalization of paid sexual self-presentation belongs in the broader story about moral and social reconfiguration.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that this pattern characterizes all young users or creators.
- It does not prove that social media alone caused the shift toward monetized sexual self-presentation.
- It does not justify collapsing all OnlyFans use into a single social meaning.
