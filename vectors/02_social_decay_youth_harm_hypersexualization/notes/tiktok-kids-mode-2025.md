# When Kids Mode Isn't For Kids: Investigating TikTok's Under 13 Experience

## Factual Summary

- This 2025 paper audits TikTok's U.S. "Under 13 Experience," or Kids Mode, to examine content curation and child-safety protections.
- The authors report that 83 percent of videos observed on the Kids Mode "For You" page were not child-directed under the paper's COPPA-based criteria, and that some inappropriate content appeared.
- The paper also says the mode lacked important safeguards such as parental controls and accessibility settings, raising design and regulatory concerns.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The abstract describes the study as an audit of TikTok's Under 13 Experience focused on content curation and safety/privacy protections.
- It reports that 83% of videos shown in Kids Mode were not child-directed.
- It states that even inappropriate content was found and that the platform lacked critical features such as parental controls and accessibility settings.
- The authors argue that these failures could push children toward the regular TikTok mode, where they face additional safety and privacy risks.

## What It Proves

- That a child-directed platform mode can still expose minors to content that the researchers did not classify as child-directed.
- That Chapter 2 can discuss platform design failures affecting minors with concrete evidence rather than only generalized concern.
- That youth-risk arguments in the dossier can include the mismatch between stated safety framing and actual curation outcomes.

## What It Suggests

- Review causal relationships carefully before elevating them into a thesis.
- That safety-by-design rhetoric may be weaker in practice than platform branding suggests.
- That youth dependency and vulnerability are shaped not only by screen time, but also by the architecture and governance of recommendation environments.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that TikTok intentionally designed Kids Mode to fail.
- It does not prove long-term psychological harm from this audit alone.
- It does not prove that TikTok is uniquely dangerous compared with every competing youth platform.
