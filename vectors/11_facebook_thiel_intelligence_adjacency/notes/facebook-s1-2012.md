# Facebook, Inc. Registration Statement on Form S-1

## Factual Summary

- Facebook's February 1, 2012 S-1 filing documents the governance structure the company presented to public investors at the time of its IPO.
- The filing identifies Peter A. Thiel as a board member who had served since April 2005 and describes him as one of Facebook's early investors.
- The filing also shows how board seats, audit-committee roles, preferred-stock designations, and dual-class voting control were arranged at the company as it entered public markets.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The filing says Peter A. Thiel had served on Facebook's board since April 2005.
- It states that Facebook believed Thiel should serve on the board because of his experience as an entrepreneur, venture capitalist, and early investor in the company.
- It states that Thiel held the board seat designated by holders of a majority of the Series A preferred stock.
- It lists Thiel as a member of Facebook's audit committee.

## What It Proves

- That Peter Thiel had a formal governance role inside Facebook at the time of the IPO filing and that the role dated back to 2005.
- That Facebook itself described Thiel not merely as a commentator or associate, but as an early investor and standing director.
- That Thiel occupied a structurally meaningful position in Facebook's governance architecture, including a preferred-stock-designated seat and an audit-committee role.
- That Facebook's public-market transition preserved concentrated internal control over directors and corporate decisions.

## What It Suggests

- That the early Facebook governance structure joined large-scale data-platform growth with venture-capital influence at the board level.
- That Thiel's relationship to Facebook can be discussed as documented governance adjacency, not rumor or retrospective mythmaking.
- That later readings of platform power should pay attention to who sat inside the governance structure, not only to the product or founder narrative.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that the CIA directly invested in Facebook.
- It does not prove that Peter Thiel personally controlled Facebook's strategic direction.
- It does not prove that Facebook was directed by intelligence agencies or that board membership itself implies covert coordination.
