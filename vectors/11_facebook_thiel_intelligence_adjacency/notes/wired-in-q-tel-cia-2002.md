# It Pays to Play With the CIA

## Factual Summary

- This 2002 Wired archival feature describes In-Q-Tel as the venture-capital arm of the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology and gives an early operational snapshot of how the model was publicly explained at the time.
- The article quotes In-Q-Tel associate vice president of corporate communications Gayle von Eckartsberg saying that In-Q-Tel was a private company with its own board and a contract from the government to find promising IT companies and build strategic relationships with them.
- The same article reports that In-Q-Tel had evaluated about 2,000 business plans, established strategic relationships with more than 20 companies, and already seen 19 projects result in products or processes deployed by the agency.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The article describes In-Q-Tel as "the venture capital arm of the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology."
- It states that In-Q-Tel was "incorporated as a not-for-profit corporation" in early 1999.
- Von Eckartsberg is quoted saying: "In-Q-Tel is a private company with our own board and a contract from the government to find companies with promising products, primarily in the IT area, and build strategic relationships with them."
- The article says In-Q-Tel had evaluated about 2,000 business plans and established strategic relationships with over 20 companies.
