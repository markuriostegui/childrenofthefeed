# Work-From-Home is Here to Stay: Call for Flexibility in Post-Pandemic Work Policies

## Factual Summary

- This paper reviews experiences from 17 companies across 12 countries after two years of predominantly work-from-home arrangements triggered by the pandemic.
- It argues that the pandemic converted work from home from an exceptional perk into a core entitlement in the expectations of many employees.
- The paper finds that companies were formalizing more flexibility in work location and time, while differing in how far they were willing to extend that flexibility.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The abstract says the pandemic forced an abrupt transition from offices to homes in early 2020.
- It reports three main findings: more formal flexibility, substantial variation across companies, and a changed psychological contract between employees and managers.
- It says work from home was converted from an exclusive perk into a core privilege that employees felt entitled to expect.

## What It Proves

- That pandemic-era remote work was not only temporary improvisation; it reshaped post-pandemic organizational policy and worker expectations.
- That COVID accelerated a durable normalization of digitally mediated work arrangements.
- That Chapter 3 can discuss the pandemic as a long-tail institutional accelerator rather than only as a short emergency episode.

## What It Suggests

- Review causal relationships carefully before elevating them into a thesis.
- That once remote mediation became normalized, employers and workers both had to renegotiate basic assumptions about time, place, and managerial control.
- That the pandemic helped embed digital mediation into the ordinary psychological contract of work.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that all sectors experienced the same transformation as the tech-oriented companies in the study.
- It does not prove that post-pandemic flexibility necessarily benefited all workers equally.
- It does not prove that remote normalization alone created broader political or cultural dependence on platforms.
