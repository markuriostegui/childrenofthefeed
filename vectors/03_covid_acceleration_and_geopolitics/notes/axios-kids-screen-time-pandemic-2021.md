# Kids' screen time up 50% during pandemic

## Factual Summary

- Axios reported in January 2021 that parent expectations around children's screen time had shifted sharply during the pandemic.
- The article says six in ten parents estimated that before the pandemic their children spent at most three hours a day with screens, while by August 2020 seven in ten estimated at least four hours a day.
- This is not a peer-reviewed longitudinal study, but it is a useful `T2` source because it provides a direct pre-pandemic versus pandemic household comparison that Chapter 3 had been missing.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- Before the pandemic, six out of ten parents said their children's screen time topped out at three hours.
- In August 2020, seven out of ten parents estimated their children were spending at least four hours a day with screens.
- Seventy-five percent of children aged three to nine said they had more screen time than the prior year.

## What It Proves

- That pandemic-era digital dependence in family life can be described with a direct pre-pandemic versus pandemic comparison rather than only with pandemic-period association data.
- That Chapter 3 can now support the claim that COVID increased everyday child screen exposure at the household level.
- That youth screen intensification during the pandemic was not only a retrospective cultural impression.

## What It Suggests

- That emergency digitization extended deeply into home life, not only into formal work or school systems.
- That later youth-dependence and mental-health concerns can be read against a documented step-change in everyday screen routines.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove direct mental-health causation on its own.
- It does not provide the rigor of a national longitudinal academic study across all screen categories.
- It does not prove that pandemic screen-time increases were uniform across every family or age bracket.
