# How Sam Altman's ouster went down, according to OpenAI's ex-president

## Factual Summary

- Axios published a contemporaneous reconstruction of Altman's firing based largely on Greg Brockman's public account and additional reporting.
- The article is useful because it captures the mechanics of the board rupture: private summons, abrupt firing, Brockman's removal from the board, and Microsoft's claim that it was caught off guard.
- In the dossier, the note supports the chronology of the crisis while keeping clear that some details come through a participant's perspective rather than neutral adjudication.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- Axios says Altman was told in a Google Meet that he was being fired and the news would go out very soon.
- The report says Brockman was removed from the board but initially told he would retain his role.
- It lists the six board members in place at the time, including Ilya Sutskever, Adam D'Angelo, Tasha McCauley, and Helen Toner.
- Axios also reports that Microsoft was blindsided and learned of Altman's ouster only shortly before the public.

## What It Proves

- That the November 2023 OpenAI crisis involved an abrupt and unusually fragile governance process.
- That Microsoft was not publicly presented as controlling the board process at the moment of Altman's firing.
- That the crisis immediately implicated board composition, partner influence, and management legitimacy.

## What It Suggests

- That the governance structure of a frontier lab can generate acute instability even when backed by enormous capital and prestige.
- That the board crisis was not only ideological; it was also procedural and organizational.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that Brockman's account is complete or uncontested.
- It does not prove hidden technical motives.
- It does not prove a long-run theory of institutional capture by itself.
