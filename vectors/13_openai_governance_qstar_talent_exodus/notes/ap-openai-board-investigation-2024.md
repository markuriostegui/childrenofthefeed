# OpenAI has 'full confidence' in CEO Sam Altman after investigation, reinstates him to board

## Factual Summary

- On March 8, 2024, AP reported that OpenAI reinstated Sam Altman to the board after an outside investigation by WilmerHale into the November 2023 crisis.
- AP said OpenAI described Altman's ouster as a consequence of a breakdown in trust with the prior board, while also saying his conduct did not mandate removal.
- The article also documented the addition of Sue Desmond-Hellmann, Nicole Seligman, and Fidji Simo, alongside governance reforms such as a whistleblower hotline and stronger conflict rules.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- AP reported that OpenAI said it had full confidence in Altman's leadership after the investigation.
- Bret Taylor said the review found a significant breakdown in trust between the prior board and Sam Altman and Greg Brockman.
- AP reported that Taylor said the firing did not arise from product safety, security, finances, or statements to investors, customers, or business partners.
- The piece said OpenAI's conflicts were rooted in its unusual nonprofit-controlled governance structure.

## What It Proves

- That OpenAI and AP both treated the November 2023 episode as a governance rupture requiring formal review and institutional repair.
- That the company's public line after the investigation distinguished governance breakdown from claims about product safety, finances, or investor misstatements.
- That the board reset was accompanied by a deliberate legitimacy rebuild through new directors and governance procedures.

## What It Suggests

- That later claims tying the November crisis exclusively to safety fears should be handled carefully because this `T2` account records a narrower official explanation.
- That OpenAI's recovery strategy relied on restoring institutional confidence rather than merely restoring the chief executive.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that the WilmerHale review was substantively independent beyond the company's description.
- It does not prove that safety concerns were absent inside the organization; it only records what Taylor said the firing did not arise from.
- It does not resolve deeper disagreements about commercialization, capability anxiety, or internal power politics.
