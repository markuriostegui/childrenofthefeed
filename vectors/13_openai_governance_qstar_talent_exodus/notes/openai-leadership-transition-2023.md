# OpenAI announces leadership transition

## Factual Summary

- On November 17, 2023, OpenAI officially announced the departure of Sam Altman as CEO and board member, and named Mira Murati interim CEO effective immediately.
- The company itself stated that the departure followed a deliberative board review and that Altman had not been “consistently candid” in his communications with the board.
- The note also reiterates OpenAI’s nonprofit/capped-profit architecture and frames the episode as a dispute over governance and mission, not as a simple operational transition.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The piece is dated November 17, 2023.
- OpenAI says Mira Murati assumes the role of interim CEO “effective immediately.”
- The board says Altman “was not consistently candid in his communications with the board.”
- The text reminds readers that OpenAI was founded as a nonprofit in 2015 and restructured in 2019 to raise capital while preserving mission and oversight.

## What It Proves

- That the November 2023 crisis was recognized by OpenAI itself as a formal governance rupture.
- That the board justified Altman’s removal in terms of candor, oversight capacity, and institutional mission.
- That OpenAI still publicly presented itself as an exceptional structure where nonprofit mission and oversight were meant to prevail over ordinary corporate logic.

## What It Suggests

- That the governance of frontier labs cannot be understood only as product management, but as an arena where mission, safety, capital, and legitimacy openly collide.
- That later narratives about alignment and safety coexist with internal conflicts over strategic control of the lab.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove any specific theory about Q* or hidden motives not stated by the board.
- It does not prove that the board’s explanation exhausts all the real causes of the conflict.
- It does not prove state coordination, though it does help map the political-corporate dimension of the actor.
