# Introducing Superalignment

## Factual Summary

- OpenAI's July 5, 2023 post announced a new Superalignment team co-led by Ilya Sutskever and Jan Leike.
- The company said it was dedicating 20% of the compute it had secured to date to solving the problem of superintelligence alignment within four years.
- The post explicitly tied frontier capabilities to governance questions, arguing that new institutions for governance would be required alongside technical breakthroughs.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- "We need scientific and technical breakthroughs to steer and control AI systems much smarter than us."
- "we're starting a new team, co-led by Ilya Sutskever and Jan Leike"
- "dedicating 20% of the compute we've secured to date"
- OpenAI said superintelligence could arrive this decade and could lead to "the disempowerment of humanity or even human extinction."

## What It Proves

- That OpenAI publicly elevated existential-risk and governance language well before the 2024 departures and 2025-2026 access fights.
- That Sutskever and Leike were formally paired in a flagship safety-governance project, which matters when later departures are interpreted.
- That Chapter 6 and Chapter 8 can document safety rhetoric as an institutional commitment with compute allocation, not only as later public-relations packaging.

## What It Suggests

- That later talent exits involving Sutskever- and Leike-linked safety figures can be read against a real prior governance/safety architecture, not a purely invented narrative.
- That OpenAI's public theory of frontier risk already fused technical control problems with broader governance claims.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that OpenAI internally behaved consistently with the ambitions of the post.
- It does not prove why any later executive left.
- It does not prove that capability anxiety directly caused the November 2023 board rupture.
