## Expected Claims

- Structural claim: the response agenda is not a menu but a three-part doctrine built around Section 230 reform, patrimony-oriented governance of frontier weights, and anti-capture obligations for AI firms.
- Implementation claim: cognitive literacy, labor power, competition policy, and public-interest infrastructure should be presented as implementation arms of those three reforms rather than as disconnected remedies.
- Historical-context claim: Paul Lara, the Vatican, BIS, and Amodei widen the response frame from technical fixes to democratic, moral, and distributional repair.
- Limitation or counterargument claim: philanthropy and personal discipline can help, but they cannot substitute for structural reform.

## Risks

- Do not let normative energy outrun the evidence base.
- Keep voluntary elite action clearly distinct from public, labor, and legal remedies.
- Do not let Chapter 10 drift back into an open-ended solution catalogue.
