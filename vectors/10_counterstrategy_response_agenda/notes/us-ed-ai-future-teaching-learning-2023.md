# Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Teaching and Learning: Insights and Recommendations

## Factual Summary

- The U.S. Department of Education's Office of Educational Technology published this report in May 2023 to frame opportunities, risks, and policy recommendations for artificial intelligence in educational settings.
- The report centers human oversight, training, transparency, and governance instead of treating AI uptake in education as an automatic good.
- It specifically links educational AI to concerns about privacy, bias, fairness, safety, and the delegation of judgment to automated systems.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The report says educational systems must govern their use of AI systems.
- It emphasizes that AI changes education when systems move from pattern detection to automated decisions affecting learning and administration.
- Its recommendations focus on policy development, educator capacity, and responsible implementation.

## What It Proves

- That an institutional response agenda can legitimately include cognitive literacy, human oversight, and training rather than relying on consumer self-control alone.
- That the response layer in Chapter 10 can draw on primary public guidance for educational governance under AI.
- That defensive measures around attention, judgment, and delegated cognition are compatible with mainstream institutional guidance.

## What It Suggests

- That Chapter 10 should frame digital hygiene and cognitive discipline as institutional practices that need support, not as isolated private virtues.
- That response to AI dependency is stronger when it includes educators, schools, and public standards.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that a single educational framework can solve broader platform or labor problems.
- It does not prove that every use of AI in education is harmful or dependency-producing.
- It does not prove that current institutions are already enforcing these safeguards effectively.
