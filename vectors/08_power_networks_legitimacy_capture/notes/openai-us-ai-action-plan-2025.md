# OpenAI’s proposals for the U.S. AI Action Plan

## Factual Summary

- On March 13, 2025, OpenAI published a public submission to the U.S. AI Action Plan process.
- The text aligns copyright posture, export controls, infrastructure buildout, and government adoption with OpenAI's own strategic vision.
- For Vector 08, the source matters because it shows a frontier lab attempting to convert market position into policy legitimacy and state partnership.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- OpenAI argues for protecting U.S. AI leadership through export controls and updated diffusion rules.
- The company asks policymakers to preserve model training on copyrighted material.
- It advocates accelerated infrastructure, energy expansion, and government use of frontier models.
- The text is written as policy advice to the U.S. state, not just as product marketing.

## What It Proves

- That OpenAI presents itself as a policy actor seeking to co-design national AI strategy.
- That the company publicly links its commercial and technical agenda to national-security and industrial-policy language.
- That Chapter 8 can treat lobbying and doctrinal positioning as part of the legitimacy architecture of frontier firms.

## What It Suggests

- That frontier firms seek institutional legitimacy not only from users and investors, but from formal alignment with state strategy.
- That the move from company to quasi-public actor is visible in official policy submissions.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that the government accepted OpenAI's full agenda.
- It does not prove unique influence over the policy process relative to rivals.
- It does not prove that the company's public-interest framing is neutral or uncontested.
