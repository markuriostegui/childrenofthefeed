# Anthropic outspends OpenAI in biggest-ever lobbying quarter

## Factual Summary

- On April 21, 2026, Axios reported that Anthropic and OpenAI both posted their biggest lobbying quarters to date in Q1 2026, citing federal lobbying disclosures and U.S. Senate data.
- The article says Anthropic spent $1.6 million and OpenAI spent $1 million in the quarter, while both companies expanded the range of policy issues they were discussing in Washington.
- The piece is useful to Chapter 08 because it turns abstract claims about policy influence into a documented spending and issue-tracking pattern.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The article is dated April 21, 2026.
- Axios says the data are from the U.S. Senate and federal lobbying disclosures.
- The article reports that Anthropic spent $1.6 million in Q1 2026 and OpenAI spent $1 million.
- It says Anthropic's listed topics included AI procurement, Defense Department procurement, supply chain risk, acceptable use policy, AI and national security, export controls, energy infrastructure, supply chain, and permitting.
- It says OpenAI's filing covered AI and copyright, cybersecurity, cloud computing, and infrastructure.

## What It Proves

- That frontier AI firms were spending meaningful and rising sums on federal lobbying by early 2026.
- That OpenAI and Anthropic were not only releasing models and policy essays, but also participating in Washington through formal lobbying channels.
- That Chapter 08 can discuss institutional influence with evidence tied to disclosed spending and issue categories, not only inferred proximity.

## What It Suggests

- That the frontier-lab legitimacy system increasingly includes routine Washington influence operations similar to mature Big Tech firms.
- That lobbying was converging with the same strategic topics already visible elsewhere in the dossier: export controls, copyright, cloud infrastructure, procurement, and national security.

## What It Does Not Prove
