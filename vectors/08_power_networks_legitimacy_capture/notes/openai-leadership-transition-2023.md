# OpenAI announces leadership transition

## Factual Summary

- On November 17, 2023, OpenAI announced Sam Altman's departure as CEO and board member and named Mira Murati interim CEO effective immediately.
- The statement presented the removal as a governance decision rooted in candor, oversight, and mission rather than as an ordinary management transition.
- For Vector 08, the importance of the source is that it shows how legitimacy in a frontier lab depends on a mission-bearing governance shell, not only on product success or investor confidence.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The statement is dated November 17, 2023.
- OpenAI says the board no longer had confidence in Altman's ability to continue leading the organization.
- The text reiterates the nonprofit origin and 2019 capped-profit restructuring.
- The company's own language frames the rupture in terms of governance and mission.

## What It Proves

- That OpenAI publicly treated its crisis as a governance and legitimacy rupture, not as a simple executive reshuffle.
- That the nonprofit-mission structure remained central to how the company justified power over leadership.
- That Chapter 8 can discuss institutional legitimacy as embedded in corporate form, board authority, and mission rhetoric.

## What It Suggests

- That frontier AI firms derive legitimacy from special-purpose governance narratives that can conflict with ordinary commercial expectations.
- That later board rebuilds and policy moves should be read as efforts to repair not only operations, but also institutional trust.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove the full internal motive behind Altman's removal.
- It does not prove that the nonprofit structure actually constrained power in practice.
- It does not prove state coordination or any specific hidden technical trigger.
