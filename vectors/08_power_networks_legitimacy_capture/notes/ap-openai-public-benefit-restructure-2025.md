# OpenAI may move forward with new business structure, partnership with Microsoft, regulators say

## Factual Summary

- On October 28, 2025, AP reported that OpenAI said it had reorganized its ownership structure and converted its business into a public benefit corporation after the Delaware and California attorneys general declined to oppose the plan.
- The article said the new arrangement left the nonprofit formally in control while giving Microsoft roughly a 27% stake in the new for-profit corporation.
- AP also reported that the restructuring preserved a Safety and Security Committee, introduced an independent expert-panel role in AGI verification, and drew criticism from Public Citizen about whether nonprofit control was real in practice.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- AP reported that Bret Taylor said the nonprofit remained in control of the for-profit and now had a direct path to major resources before AGI arrives.
- The article said OpenAI's board promised the right to appoint and remove the public benefit corporation's board members.
- AP reported that critics argued nonprofit control could be illusory even if it remained formally intact.
- The piece said Microsoft's rights to OpenAI's confidential research methods would remain until either AGI was verified by an expert panel or 2030.

## What It Proves

- That OpenAI's legitimacy architecture expanded beyond rhetoric into a regulator-reviewed public benefit restructuring.
- That nonprofit control, public-purpose language, and large-scale commercial financing were being explicitly combined in the same corporate design.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that the public benefit structure resolved the underlying conflict between safety, mission, and profit.
