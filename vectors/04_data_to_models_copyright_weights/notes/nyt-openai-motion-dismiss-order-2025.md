# The New York Times Company v. Microsoft Corporation et al, Opinion on Motions to Dismiss

## Factual Summary

- This April 4, 2025 opinion on motions to dismiss in The New York Times Company v. Microsoft Corporation et al allowed core copyright claims against OpenAI and Microsoft to proceed.
- The order is valuable because it is a second primary court document outside the Anthropic litigation, giving Chapter 4 another direct legal record about training-stage copying and liability theories.
- The opinion describes a training stage involving collection, storage, scraping, and dataset creation, and it also addresses allegations that outputs can reproduce plaintiffs' works.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- Judge Sidney H. Stein issued the opinion on April 4, 2025.
- The order denied key motions to dismiss direct and contributory copyright claims.
- The opinion describes a training stage in which plaintiffs' works were collected, stored, scraped, and incorporated into datasets used to train large language models.
- The opinion also addresses allegations that outputs may reproduce or regurgitate copyrighted material.

## What It Proves

- That another federal court outside the Anthropic case treated training-stage copying and dataset assembly as central issues serious enough to survive dismissal.
- That Chapter 4 can rely on more than one primary court record when arguing that the AI copyright conflict has split into doctrinal and supply-chain layers.
- That the legal record does not treat training as a purely abstract statistical process detached from source acquisition and storage practices.

## What It Suggests

- That large AI firms face parallel legal pressure not only over fair use, but over the concrete mechanics of how training corpora are built and retained.
- That the conflict over model training is likely to keep turning on both technical process and market substitution concerns.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that OpenAI or Microsoft will ultimately lose on the merits.
- It does not prove that all AI training is unlawful.
- It does not settle the fair-use question across every jurisdiction, dataset, or model pipeline.
