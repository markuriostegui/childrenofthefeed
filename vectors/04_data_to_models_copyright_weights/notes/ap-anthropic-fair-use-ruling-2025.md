# Anthropic wins ruling on AI training in copyright lawsuit but must face trial on pirated books

## Factual Summary

According to AP's report on June 24, 2025, a federal judge ruled that Anthropic's use of copyrighted books for AI training could qualify as fair use in a major authors' lawsuit, while separately allowing the case to proceed to trial over allegations that Anthropic obtained books from pirate libraries. The piece presents a split outcome: a meaningful judicial win for training-related fair-use arguments, but not a clean exoneration of the company’s acquisition practices.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- AP reports that the judge described the training use as "quintessentially transformative."
- AP also reports that Anthropic still had to face trial over allegations tied to pirated books or shadow-library acquisition.
- The article frames the case as potentially influential for similar lawsuits against other AI companies.

## What It Proves

- That by June 24, 2025, the legal conflict around AI training had already split into distinct layers: training-use doctrine on one side and input-acquisition legality on the other.
- That at least one major U.S. ruling treated training itself as potentially fair use while leaving piracy-related exposure alive.
- That Chapter 4 can distinguish between the legality of model training and the legality of how training libraries were assembled.

## What It Suggests

For Chapter 4, this strengthens the argument that the political economy of AI training cannot be reduced to a simple "training is legal" or "training is theft" binary. Even where training arguments gain judicial traction, the supply chain of how copyrighted material was assembled remains a separate and materially important conflict.

## What It Does Not Prove

This AP report does not itself settle the broader fair-use debate across all jurisdictions, all models, or all datasets. It also does not prove Anthropic liable on the piracy-related allegations; those allegations remained for trial at the time of the report.
