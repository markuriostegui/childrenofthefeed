# Bartz et al. v. Anthropic PBC, Order on Fair Use

## Factual Summary

This is Judge William Alsup's June 23, 2025 order on fair use in `Bartz et al. v. Anthropic PBC`, No. C 24-05417 WHA, in the Northern District of California. The order distinguishes between Anthropic's use of books to train large language models and Anthropic's acquisition and retention of millions of pirated books in a permanent internal library, holding that the training use of lawfully acquired books was fair use while refusing to excuse piracy or the creation of a permanent general-purpose pirate library.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The order says Anthropic sought a central library of "all the books in the world" to retain "forever."
- It describes the training use as "quintessentially transformative."
- It also states that Anthropic had no entitlement to use pirated copies for its central library.

## What It Proves

- That the strongest primary legal record already separates the question of model training from the question of how the training corpus was assembled.
- That by June 23, 2025, a federal court had accepted a fair-use defense for Anthropic's training on lawfully acquired books while preserving exposure for piracy-related conduct.
- That Chapter 4 can rely on a primary court order, not only press summaries, when arguing that AI copyright conflict has split into doctrinal and supply-chain layers.

## What It Suggests

For Chapter 4, this order sharpens the political-economy argument: even if some courts accept training as transformative, the economic and legal conflict may reappear at the level of acquisition, storage, licensing, and the scale at which firms assemble permanent private libraries from others' work.

## What It Does Not Prove

This order does not establish a universal rule for every AI company, every dataset, or every jurisdiction. It also does not excuse Anthropic's alleged piracy, does not resolve damages, and does not prove that all uses of copyrighted works in AI training are lawful.
