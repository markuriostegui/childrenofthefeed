# Copyright and Artificial Intelligence; Notice of Inquiry

## Factual Summary

- This August 30, 2023 Federal Register notice launched the U.S. Copyright Office's inquiry into AI and copyright.
- The notice states that the Office was studying legal and policy issues raised by AI systems, including training on copyrighted works, transparency around training inputs, and the status of AI-generated outputs.
- It formally requested public comments on how training materials are used, retained, disclosed, licensed, and potentially removed or “unlearned.”

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The summary says the Office seeks comment on “the use of copyrighted works to train AI models,” “transparency and disclosure,” and “the legal status of AI-generated outputs.”
- The notice frames generative AI as producing outputs based on learning statistical patterns in existing data that may include copyrighted works.
- The questions section asks about how training materials are used, reproduced, stored, licensed, and whether it is feasible to identify, remove, or unlearn specific materials.

## What It Proves

- That by August 2023 the U.S. Copyright Office had already elevated AI training inputs and disclosure questions into a formal federal inquiry.
- That the legal and policy debate around training data was not retrofitted later; it was a live official issue near the start of the generative AI boom.
- That Chapter 4 can document a formal institutional path from open questions about AI training to later official analysis and policy conflict.

## What It Suggests

- Review causal relationships carefully before elevating them into a thesis.
- That the state already recognized transparency, disclosure, and licensing as fault lines in AI development.
- That the dossier can frame AI enclosure as a process of institutional contestation, not just a clash of private opinions.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not resolve whether training on copyrighted works is fair use.
- It does not prove that developers can feasibly disclose every training input in practice.
- It does not prove any single preferred legislative outcome.
