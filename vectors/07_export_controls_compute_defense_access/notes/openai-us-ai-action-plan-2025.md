# OpenAI’s proposals for the U.S. AI Action Plan

## Factual Summary

- On March 13, 2025, OpenAI published its proposals for the U.S. AI Action Plan and presented them as recommendations to the White House OSTP.
- The text articulates an explicit alignment among corporate leadership, industrial policy, copyright, export controls, infrastructure, and government adoption.
- It is valuable because it shows in primary language how a frontier lab tries to shape public policy on access, intellectual property, and geopolitical competition in AI.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The piece is dated March 13, 2025.
- OpenAI proposes that export controls protect U.S. leadership and update the AI diffusion rule.
- The text asks to preserve U.S. models’ ability to learn from copyrighted material so as not to concede advantage to the PRC.
- It also calls for accelerated infrastructure, energy expansion, and state adoption of frontier tools.

## What It Proves

- That OpenAI officially defended a public-policy agenda combining global commercial expansion, export controls, and preservation of access to copyrighted material for training.
- That the company presents itself not only as a model provider, but as an actor seeking to co-design U.S. national AI strategy.
- That the language of national security, competition with the PRC, and government adoption is already integrated into its official 2025 discourse.

## What It Suggests

- That the conflict over copyright is not only legal or cultural, but also geopolitical and industrial.
- That frontier labs try to convert their technical and economic convenience into state doctrine.
- That the rhetoric of freedom can function as a legitimizing frame for consolidating privileged access, infrastructure, and regulatory advantage.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove that the government adopted these proposals in full.
- It does not prove on its own that OpenAI controls public policy; only that it tried to influence it explicitly.
- It does not prove that the copyright argument is legally correct; it proves the company’s political position.
