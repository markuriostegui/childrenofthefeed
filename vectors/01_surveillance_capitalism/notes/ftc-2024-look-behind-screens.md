# A Look Behind the Screens: Examining the Data Practices of Social Media and Video Streaming Services

## Factual Summary

- The FTC documents that major social and video platforms operate through an intensive logic of surveillance, retention, and commercial exploitation of data.
- The report works as a regulatory source for describing practices of collection, modeling, and monetization that go beyond the idea of a neutral social network.
- It is a central piece for grounding Chapter 1 in institutional evidence rather than only cultural or journalistic criticism.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- It examines the data practices of social media and video streaming platforms.
- It anchors the discussion in mass collection, engagement-oriented design, and opaque uses of data.
- It strengthens the bridge between commercial surveillance and the preparation of behavioral datasets.

## What It Proves

- That the platform model relies on intensive extraction and processing of user data.
- That platforms cannot seriously be described as simple passive intermediaries.
- That there is regulatory support for speaking of commercial surveillance at massive scale.

## What It Suggests

- That the free capture of social behavior precedes and feeds the later development of AI systems trained on large human corpora.
- That engagement optimization and data exploitation are parts of the same architecture of power.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove by itself that those data were directly used to train specific frontier models.
- It does not prove state or intelligence coordination.
- It does not exhaust the political dimension of the problem; it only anchors the regulatory and commercial layer.
