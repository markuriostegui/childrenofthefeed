# In-Q-Tel

## Factual Summary

- The official In-Q-Tel homepage describes the organization as a global not-for-profit investment platform aimed at introducing strategic technologies to government partners.
- The site states that IQT has produced mission impact for more than a quarter century and lists mission partners such as the CIA, NGA, FBI, DIA, NSA, NRO, DHS, U.S. Cyber Command, and U.S. Space Force.
- This source documents the explicit existence of a venture-intelligence infrastructure around frontier technologies and national security.

## Key Quotes or Findings

- The site presents itself with the line “investing in global innovation to secure the nation.”
- IQT describes itself as a “not-for-profit global investment platform.”
- It states that it accelerates the introduction of technologies that enhance the national security and prosperity of the United States and its allies.
- It publicly lists intelligence and security agencies as mission partners.

## What It Proves

- That there is an organization officially dedicated to connecting technological innovation with intelligence, defense, and national-security needs.
- That adjacency between venture capital, frontier tech, and the state apparatus is not speculation: in the case of IQT, it is expressed by the organization itself.
- That the dossier can discuss a technology-transfer infrastructure toward the state without depending on conspiratorial sources.

## What It Suggests

- That the boundary between commercial innovation and national security has become increasingly porous and strategic.
- That any reading of platforms, venture capital, and state power should include bridge organizations of this kind, while avoiding unsupported causal leaps.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove a specific IQT or CIA investment in Facebook.
- It does not prove concrete personal relationships with Peter Thiel or a closed plot around a specific case.
- It does not prove that every relevant tech firm is coordinated by intelligence.
