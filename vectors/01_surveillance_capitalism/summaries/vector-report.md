## Hypotheses / Interpretations

- That sovereign-scale platforms should be regulated on the basis of design, amplification, targeting, monetization, moderation architecture, and systemic reach rather than passive-hosting logic.

## Speculative Narrative Risks

- That refactoring Section 230 for sovereign-scale platforms requires treating platforms as ordinary publishers for every user utterance regardless of design, amplification, targeting, or monetization.

## Reform-Case Synthesis

- Proves for reform case: sovereign-scale platforms were built around intentional extraction, retention, monetization, and governance of user behavior at a scale incompatible with the old passive-intermediary story.
- Suggests for reform case: once recommendation, targeting, monetization, and moderation architecture are treated as product design rather than background hosting, the strongest legal destination is a refactored Section 230 keyed to systemic reach and design responsibility.
- Overreach boundary: this vector does not by itself prove a fully mapped pipeline from every platform dataset to every frontier-model training stack, nor does it justify blanket publisher liability for all user speech.
- Materially strengthens: Chapters `01` and `10`, with structural support for Chapters `07` and `08` through the governance-adjacency layer.
