## Main Analysis

The GPTs paper and the IMF note both support an uneven future in which exposure to generative AI is distributed asymmetrically across occupations, income positions, and labor systems. The future problem is not uniform extinction. It is differential vulnerability. ^[Sources: arXiv, [GPTs are GPTs: An Early Look at the L...](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130) (2023-03-17; see [Appendix A2](#appendix-a2-gpts-are-gpts-an-early-look-at-the-labor-market-impact-potential-of-large-language-models)); IMF, [Gen-AI: Artificial Intelligence and t...](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/staff-discussion-notes/issues/2024/01/14/gen-ai-artificial-intelligence-and-the-future-of-work-542379) (2024-01-14; see [Appendix A4](#appendix-a4-gen-ai-artificial-intelligence-and-the-future-of-work))]

Compute-governance research supports the claim that high-end capability is likely to remain infrastructure-concentrated unless policy changes the terms of access. That means unequal access to machine intelligence is already an infrastructure question, not only a talent question. ^[Sources: arXiv, [Computing Power and the Governance of...](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797) (2024-02-13; see [Appendix A3](#appendix-a3-computing-power-and-the-governance-of-artificial-intelligence))]

The continuity scenario also includes unequal fallback. Some of the same actors asking institutions and publics to normalize deeper dependence on AI and platform infrastructure have also publicly described or been credibly reported as building private redundancy, jurisdictional exit, and survivability options: Altman's 2016 prep disclosures and New Zealand fallback, Zuckerberg's Kauai shelter complex, and Thiel's long association with New Zealand retreat logic and elite-exit politics. That does not prove foreknowledge, coordinated collapse engineering, or a hidden command chain. It does show how continuity can stratify not only access to intelligence, but access to backup, redundancy, and escape when shared systems fail or become politically untrustworthy. ^[Sources: Tad Friend, [Sam Altman's Manifest Destiny](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2016/10/10/sam-altmans-manifest-destiny) (2016-10-03; see [Appendix A5](#appendix-a5-sam-altman-s-manifest-destiny)); Makena Kelly, [Inside Mark Zuckerberg’s Top-Secret H...](https://www.wired.com/story/mark-zuckerberg-inside-hawaii-compound) (2023-12-14; see [Appendix A6](#appendix-a6-inside-mark-zuckerbergs-top-secret-hawaii-compound)); Charlotte Phillipp, [Mark Zuckerberg Downplays His 5,000-S...](https://people.com/mark-zuckerberg-hawaii-bunker-5000-square-feet-8765376) (2024-12-20; see [Appendix A7](#appendix-a7-mark-zuckerberg-downplays-his-5-000-square-foot-underground-bunker-in-hawaii-just-a-little-shelter)); Mark O'Connell, [Why Silicon Valley billionaires are p...](https://www.theguardian.com/news/2018/feb/15/why-silicon-valley-billionaires-are-prepping-for-the-apocalypse-in-new-zealand) (2018-02-15; see [Appendix A8](#appendix-a8-why-silicon-valley-billionaires-are-prepping-for-the-apocalypse-in-new-zealand)); Rebecca Rose, [New Zealand woos the wealthy with ‘go...](https://www.ft.com/content/76ca455c-3b16-4413-8738-3f17431d125b) (2025-09-24; see [Appendix A9](#appendix-a9-new-zealand-woos-the-wealthy-with-golden-visa-changes))]

The financial and moral-warning layers also matter. The BIS warned of fragile capex dynamics and weak near-term returns in the AI buildout, while Dario Amodei publicly warned that AI-era wealth concentration could strain democratic society. These are not identical claims, but together they strengthen the dossier's continuity scenario of hierarchy rather than broad emancipation. ^[Sources: Bank for International Settlements, [Annual Economic Report 2026](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2026e.pdf) (2026-06-28; see [Appendix A10](#appendix-a10-annual-economic-report-2026)); Dario Amodei, [The Adolescence of Technology](https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology) (2026-01-01; see [Appendix A11](#appendix-a11-the-adolescence-of-technology))]

Interpretation: if current platform habits, labor structures, weights enclosure, compute chokepoints, and asymmetric fallback systems continue reinforcing one another, the likely future is not total machine takeover but a regime of dependency, access hierarchy, and weaker cognitive sovereignty. A contextual formulation from Dion Wiggins is useful here: continuity can disguise dependency when systems still appear to function while effective authority migrates elsewhere. That is precisely why the dossier's three reforms belong together rather than as isolated policy tweaks. ^[Sources: FTC, [A Look Behind the Screens: Examining...](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/look-behind-screens-examining-data-practices-social-media-video-streaming-services) (n.d.; see [Appendix A12](#appendix-a12-a-look-behind-the-screens-examining-the-data-practices-of-social-media-and-video-streaming-services)); Meta, [Mark Zuckerberg's Message to Meta Emp...](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/03/mark-zuckerberg-meta-year-of-efficiency/) (2023-03-14; see [Appendix A13](#appendix-a13-mark-zuckerberg-s-message-to-meta-employees)); arXiv, [Computing Power and the Governance of...](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797) (2024-02-13; see [Appendix A3](#appendix-a3-computing-power-and-the-governance-of-artificial-intelligence)); Dario Amodei, [The Adolescence of Technology](https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology) (2026-01-01; see [Appendix A11](#appendix-a11-the-adolescence-of-technology)); Dion Wiggins, [The Digital Sovereignty Imperative](https://digitalsovereigntyimperative.com/) (n.d.; see [Appendix A14](#appendix-a14-the-digital-sovereignty-imperative))]
